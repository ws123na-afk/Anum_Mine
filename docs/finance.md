# Finance: your own ledger, safe-to-spend, Guardian rules, read-only connectors, paper trading and the execution gateway

Finance is ANUM's native Finance domain, stages 2 to 9: one person's own ledger and a read-only "Financial Twin" built from the bank and card statements they import (stage 2), what is safe to spend for the rest of the month (stage 3), the person's own spending and risk rules with the Guardian policy kernel that every finance action must pass (stage 4), read-only connectors that bring outside data in (stage 5), SIMULATED paper and shadow trading against prices the person recorded (stage 6), the execution gateway, the one governed path any real action would have to take (stage 7), and the frameworks for limited live (stage 8) and wider live use (stage 9), both **shipped off**. It answers "what did I spend this month", "which bills are due", "what do I owe" and "how much can I still spend", and lets the person practise orders with simulated money. Since November 2026 one live path exists, for the owner's own Interactive Brokers account only: owner-approved limit orders for US-listed ETFs, paper first, behind every lock in [Live trading with Interactive Brokers](finance-live-trading.md). Everything else on this page that moves money stays refused.

Code: `services/api/anum_api/finance/` (`safe_to_spend.py`, `fx.py`, `guardian.py`, `guardian_review.py`, `policy.py`, `connectors.py`, `sync.py`, `plan_api.py` for stages 3 to 5; `trading.py`, `execution.py`, `gateway.py`, `live.py`, `wider.py`, `trading_api.py`, `wider_api.py` for stages 6 to 9), `services/api/anum_api/db/finance_repository.py`, migrations `0056_finance_ledger`, `0057_finance_plan_guard_conn`, `0058_finance_paper_exec`, `0059_finance_wider_live` and `0140_finance_guardian_reviews`, `packages/contracts/src/finance.ts`, web `apps/web/src/FinanceView.tsx`, `FinancePlan.tsx`, `FinanceGuardian.tsx`, `FinanceTrading.tsx` and `FinanceWider.tsx`, Flutter `apps/mobile/lib/features/finance/`.

Bank and card SMS forwarded from an iPhone, the review queue and the double-entry journal with its ledger dashboard are in [iPhone SMS banking and the double-entry ledger](finance-sms.md) (off by default). A transaction an SMS recorded is part of a journal entry: the Transactions tab cannot change its amount, date or category (correct it under iPhone SMS, Review), and deleting it reverses the whole entry.

## Hard limits

These are rules of the design, not settings:

- **No real money moves outside the IBKR path.** The only order call is the IBKR executor ([live trading](finance-live-trading.md)): limit orders for allowed US ETFs, each approved by the owner, inside hard limits, with every live gate. There is no payment, transfer or withdrawal call anywhere. The Guardian kernel's `gate` denies every real money-moving action whatever the rules say (`execution_not_available`); only SIMULATED paper and shadow orders run, and they touch no account.
- **Live is off by default.** Only the IBKR executor can lift it, and only with `ANUM_FINANCE_LIVE_BROKER=ibkr`, the paper proof, the owner's typed code and a cooling-off ([locks](finance-live-trading.md#modes-and-the-live-locks)). Otherwise: No live executor and no write-scoped connector exist. `ANUM_FINANCE_LIVE_ENABLED` and `ANUM_FINANCE_WIDER_LIVE_ENABLED` are refused at startup (and by the Helm chart), the gateway refuses every live request ("Live execution is not enabled"), and the database refuses a live request in any approved or executed state.
- **No keys to the AI.** The assistant reads the ledger only through read-only tools (and may propose a stricter rule). A connector's credentials are encrypted at rest and never reach a prompt, a tool result, agent memory, a log, an audit record, the browser or an export.
- **Read only connectors.** Every connector scope ends in `:read`, enforced by a database check constraint; no adapter has a write, order or transfer call. Third-party data is untrusted input.
- **The person's rules are the person's.** An agent or plugin can read the rules and propose a stricter change; it can never confirm a change, and a proposal that loosens anything is refused for agents (also by a database check). Every change is confirmed by the person with the payload hash shown.
- **Every request is the person's to approve.** An assistant may propose a SIMULATED order or a wider-live step; it can never approve, execute, turn live on, set live limits, change a kill switch or grant itself more authority. Approving needs the person's own session, the payload hash they were shown, once, before it expires.
- **No profit promises.** ANUM reports what the statements say. It gives no investment advice and makes no forecast of returns. Simulated results are labelled SIMULATED everywhere, with the fee and slippage assumptions, and say nothing certain about what a real order would earn or lose.
- **No market prices.** No market-price source is built (it needs a licensed provider). Holdings are quantities and costs the person types; paper orders fill only at prices the person records (`finance_prices`) or, for currencies, at the recorded exchange rates. The only fetched data is exchange rates from the European Central Bank.

A request to pay or transfer ("pay the DEWA bill", "transfer 500 to the landlord") is not a finance question: the finance planner returns nothing for it and the request goes to the governed `anum.external-action` skill, which waits for an approval like every other external action.

## Money rules

- Amounts are exact: `Decimal` in Python, `numeric(20, 4)` in PostgreSQL, each next to a three-letter ISO 4217 `currency`. Holdings' quantities are `numeric(28, 8)`.
- **The one rounding rule:** an amount is rounded once, when it enters ANUM (a CSV cell, a form field), to its currency's minor unit with banker's rounding (`ROUND_HALF_EVEN`): 2 decimals for AED and most currencies, 3 for BHD, IQD, JOD, KWD, LYD, OMR and TND, 0 for JPY, KRW and a few others. Sums of rounded amounts are exact and never rounded again (`finance/money.py`).
- Different currencies are never added together. Every total (balances, spend, income, net, bills due, debts) is a list of `{currency, amount}`.
- On the wire an amount is a decimal string (`"1234.50"`), so no client turns it into a binary float. Clients format it for display only.
- Signs: a transaction's amount is positive for money in and negative for money out. A bill, debt balance, goal and cost are positive.
- **FX, honestly.** Amounts are never converted silently. A converted figure exists only in safe-to-spend's optional total in the base currency, only when every rate needed is recorded and recent, and each conversion shows its rate, date and source ([Exchange rates](#exchange-rates)). There is still no cross-currency "net worth".

## Privacy

- Every finance table (migrations `0056` to `0059`) except the organisation's kill switch has a foreign key to its workspace, **forced** RLS, the standard `tenant_isolation_<table>` policy and a **restrictive** `owner_only_<table>` policy: `owner_user_id = nullif(current_setting('anum.user_id', true), '')`. Restrictive policies are AND-ed with the permissive one, so a row is visible and writable only to its owner inside its own tenant and workspace. With no `anum.user_id` set nothing is visible. No role is granted anything new and no job reads finance across tenants or people ([Multi-tenancy](multi-tenancy.md)).
- The store opens its session with `open_scoped_store(..., user_scoped=True)` and repeats the tenant, workspace and owner in every query.
- Audit records for finance changes carry the action and the record id only (plus rule codes, version numbers, provider names and counts): never an amount, a rule's limit, a description, an account name or number, or a credential.
- The background sync and key rotation find connections through `anum_maintenance`, which reads only id, scope, owner, `enabled`, `status` and `next_sync_at` of due connections (or of connections holding credentials, for rotation). For `finance_connections` the restrictive owner policy also admits a session whose `current_user` is `anum_maintenance` (a login that merely inherits the role never is); all work then runs as the application role in the owner's own scope.
- `finance_exec_global` holds only whether the organisation's kill switch is on, who changed it and when, under forced RLS on `tenant_id`.
- "Download my data" exports the whole ledger (section `finance`, including the safe-to-spend settings, rule versions and changes, connections and rates, recorded prices, paper and shadow portfolios and orders, execution requests, execution settings, wider-live proposals and incidents) and "Delete my data" deletes it (count `finance_records`) ([Privacy](privacy.md)). A connection's credentials are never exported, not even encrypted: only `has_credentials`.
- Statements are not kept: only the parsed rows and the file's hash.
- The six tables of [Rules and learning](#rules-and-learning) (`finance_rules`, `finance_merchant_aliases`, `finance_subcategories`, `finance_suggestion_decisions`, `finance_rule_batches`, `finance_automation_settings`, migration `0152_finance_rules_learning`) have the same forced RLS with the tenant, owner-only and purge policies, are exported (`finance_rules`, `merchant_aliases`, `subcategories`, `suggestion_decisions`, `rule_batches`, `automation_policy`) and deleted with the rest of Finance, and purged with a deleted workspace or organisation.

## CSV import

PDF statements (text, scanned or password protected) are read and reviewed on the same surface and recorded through the same path, below: [PDF bank statements](finance-statements.md).

`POST /api/v1/finance/imports/preview` reads a file and shows what an import would do; nothing is saved. `POST /api/v1/finance/imports` sends the same file and choices again and writes the new rows in one unit of work. Both take `{account_id, filename, content, preset?, date_format?, mapping?}`, where `content` is the file's text.

- **Layouts.** A generic layout (a date, a description, and either one signed amount column or a debit and a credit column; English or Arabic column names) and presets for common UAE bank exports: Emirates NBD, First Abu Dhabi Bank, ADCB, Mashreq and Dubai Islamic Bank. A preset is a set of column names written from typical exports; they are not certified against every bank's current file. When a file uses other names the preview says so and the person picks the columns (`mapping`).
- **Preamble lines.** The header row is found within the first 20 rows, so account details above the table do not break the import.
- **Dates.** The format is detected from every date in the file (rows that are no date at all, such as a footer, are ignored for detection and reported as unread). When the dates fit both day first and month first, ANUM takes day first (the UAE convention) and flags it; `date_format` overrides it.
- **Amounts.** `1,234.50`, `1.234,50`, `-12.00`, `(12.00)`, `12.00 DR`, `5.00 CR`, `AED 9`, Arabic-Indic digits.
- **Limits.** 2 MB, 5,000 rows, 50 columns, 1,000 characters a cell, 500 characters a description. Larger files are refused with what to do ("export one month at a time").
- **Untrusted input.** The file is text only: never evaluated or opened by a spreadsheet program. Descriptions are stripped of control, format and direction-override characters. Nothing in a file can choose an account, a tool or a rule.
- **Duplicates.** Each row's hash covers the account, date, amount, currency, cleaned description and its occurrence number among identical rows in the same file. The hash is unique per account, so importing the same or an overlapping statement again adds only new rows; two identical purchases on one day in one file stay two rows. Beyond the hash, every row goes through the [statement matching engine](#statement-matching): rows that SMS, Apple Pay or the person already recorded are attached to that record instead of being added, and the preview groups rows by what the import will do.
- **Running balance and reference.** A `balance` column (the balance after the row) feeds the [balance check](#reconciliation); a `reference` column is the bank's reference, used by the matching engine. Both are detected from common names and can be mapped.
- **Balances.** `opening_balance` and `closing_balance` (optional, as printed on the statement) are checked against the rows.
- **Export.** `GET /api/v1/finance/transactions/export.csv` (same filters as the list, at most 10,000 rows). Text cells that start with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, `|`, `%`, a tab or a carriage return get a leading apostrophe so a spreadsheet does not run them as formulas.

## Statement matching

One engine reads every statement row against what the person's ledger already holds, for CSV and PDF alike (`finance/statement_match.py`, pure; `finance/statement_ledger.py` reads and writes the stores; `finance/statement_import.py` holds the views and the transfer routes). It runs when the preview is built and again, on the live ledger and under the owner's lock, when the rows are imported, so a preview that went stale cannot double count. It compares each row with the records on that account from any source: an earlier statement, an iPhone SMS, Apple Pay, an entry the person made (including installment payments once they are in the ledger). Reversal entries are not records of their own.

Every row gets one status, with a reason code and the record it matched:

| Status | When | What the import does by default |
|---|---|---|
| `exact_duplicate` | The same statement row was imported or confirmed before (same dedupe hash, including rows that only confirmed an SMS), is waiting in Review from an earlier import, or another statement already recorded the same day, amount and merchant (`same_statement_row`) | Left out. A `same_statement_row` duplicate may be ticked to import anyway; a row seen before by its hash never can |
| `matched_existing` | An SMS, Apple Pay or hand-entered record of the same purchase | No new transaction: the row is attached to the record as a `statement` source and the record is marked reconciled. If the bank billed a different amount for the same foreign purchase, the record is corrected to the billed amount (unless unticked) |
| `possible_duplicate` | Same amount and dates but a merchant says nothing (only generic banking words, or no text), or two different records fit, or the merchants disagree while the row prints the record's own time (within 30 minutes). Two merchants that disagree with nothing stronger are two purchases: `new` | Held in Finance, iPhone SMS, Review (`ambiguous_match`). Ticked: imported as a separate payment. Skipped: left out |
| `new` | Nothing recorded fits | Imported. Unticked: left out |

### How matching works

Stages run strongest first and stop at the first one that gives a unique, one to one answer; anything ambiguous goes to the person.

| Stage | Rule | Window |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Same row | Dedupe hash (account, date, amount, currency, cleaned description, occurrence) already in the ledger or in an attached or waiting statement row | Same date |
| 1. Identifier | The bank's reference on the row agrees with the record's; a conflicting reference rules the record out | Statement posts 0 to 3 days after the record |
| 2. Amount and merchant | Same direction, amount and currency, and the merchant words agree | 0 to 3 days |
| 2b. Foreign amount | The same original foreign amount and currency (the row's text or columns against the SMS reading), the billed amounts within 10 % of each other, merchants not disagreeing | 0 to 3 days |
| 3. Weak | Same amount and window, no merchant name to compare (or a disagreeing one at the record's own printed time) | Never attached: `possible_duplicate` |

- **Descriptors are normalized first:** card numbers, masked digits, dates and times, `POS`/`VIS`/`DEB`/`MADA` style prefixes and common city and country suffixes are removed before words are compared, and a word with digits in it (a reference such as `88AB12`) is not a merchant word.
- **Merchants agree only on a meaningful word** (`finance/merchant_match.py`, the one helper the SMS and Apple Pay matcher, the statement engine, bills, transfers and the register's possible-duplicate signal all use): at least three letters, no digits, not a generic banking word in English or Arabic (`purchase`, `payment`, `transfer`, `online`, `pos`, `mada`, `شراء`, `حوالة`, `سداد`, `دفع`, `نقاط البيع`, `بطاقة ائتمانية` and the like, `STOP_WORDS`). Two rows that share only "online purchase" or "حوالة داخلية" are not the same merchant, so another statement's `ONLINE PURCHASE SAMPLE BOOKS` is never taken for `ONLINE PURCHASE EXAMPLE CAFE`. The Arabic article is ignored (`النخلة` and `نخلة` agree).
- **One window for "the same payment":** `SAME_PAYMENT_DAYS` (3) is the statement engine's posting lag and the register's possible-duplicate window.
- **One to one.** A record absorbs at most one statement row. Rows are taken in date then line order, candidates in a fixed order (fewest days apart, most words shared, oldest), so the answer is the same every time.
- **Two identical purchases on one day stay two rows.** Two records nobody could tell apart (same day, amount and merchant) are interchangeable, so two such rows match two such records; with one record, the second row is `new`. Two records that differ (other days) make the row `possible_duplicate`. A PDF row's printed time can pick between them when exactly one is within 30 minutes.
- **No amount tolerance** for same-currency matches: amounts are equal or they are not. The only tolerance is for the same foreign purchase billed differently, and that difference is shown and corrected, never absorbed silently.
- **Corrections go through the audited path.** A hand-entered or statement record is reposted in its canonical chain (as `PATCH /transactions` does); an SMS or Apple Pay record is reversed and posted again from its message with the original amount and the billed amount (as Finance, iPhone SMS, Review does). The journal keeps both entries; the import's audit row counts `corrected`.
- **One row for several recorded payments** is offered, never applied on its own: see [below](#one-row-for-several-recorded-payments).

### One row for several recorded payments

Sometimes a bank books several payments as one statement row (two SMS purchases of 100 and 50 at one shop appear as one row of 150). For a row that is `new` or `possible_duplicate`, the engine also offers `splits`: groups of 2 to 4 recorded payments on the account (`statement_match.split_options`) that

- add up to the row **exactly** (no tolerance), in the same direction and currency;
- were recorded 0 to 3 days before the row (`SAME_PAYMENT_DAYS`), each smaller than the row;
- are SMS, Apple Pay or hand-entered records with a journal entry, not already confirmed by a statement and not paired as an own transfer, and not taken by another row of the statement;
- have merchants that do not disagree with the row's.

At most the 12 nearest records are looked at and at most 3 groups are offered, ranked by how many merchants agree, then fewer records, then the nearest days, so the answer is the same every time. Such a row is listed under **Needs your decision**. Nothing is applied unless the person ticks a group (`decisions[].split` for a CSV, `rows[].split` for a PDF: the group's `record_id`s).

At import the engine runs again on the live ledger under the owner's lock. **All or nothing:** the row is attached only when the ticked group is still exactly one the engine offers and none of its records is used by another row of this import; then the row is kept as a statement source of each record (one `statement` message per record, its part's amount, the group id, the row's line) and every record is marked reconciled; nothing new is added to the ledger. Otherwise none of them is attached and the row is held in Finance, iPhone SMS, Review (`split_changed`). The import counts it as reconciled (`split_matched` on the import) and lists the row as `attached_to_existing` with `reason: split_match`, `split_group` and `split_records`.

**Undo.** `POST /api/v1/finance/imports/{import_id}/splits/{group}/undo` (`finance:manage`) detaches every record of the group together (each loses its reconciled mark unless another statement row confirms it too) and the row waits in Review, where it is imported as its own payment or left out; the import's counts and the row's disposition (`held_for_review`, `split_undone`) follow. Import history shows **Undo the match** on such a row. Undoing the whole import detaches its groups too ([Undoing an import](#undoing-an-import)). Audit: `finance.import.split_undone` (ids and counts). The phone points to the web for imports, so this is web only. Tests: `services/api/tests/test_finance_statement_split.py`, `test_postgres_finance_split_unpair.py`, `apps/web/test/financeSplitMatch.test.ts`, `apps/web/e2e/finance-statement-match.spec.ts`.

The preview answers, for every row, `match` (`status`, `code`, `reason` in English, `record`, `candidates`, `lag_days`, `amount_difference`, `bill`, `transfer`, `default_import`) and `fx`, plus `counts` per status. The import takes the person's choices as `decisions: [{line, action: "import" | "skip", bill: false, transfer: true | false, correct: false}]` (CSV, by line) or the same fields on each PDF row, and answers `reconciled`, `held_for_review`, `corrected`, `skipped`, `bills_paid`, `bills_review` and `transfers` next to the counts it always had.

### FX details from the row text

`finance/fx_text.py` is the one reader of money in words, shared with the SMS parser (it holds the currency words, the amount pattern and the "second amount in another currency is the billed one" rule). For a statement row it reads, from the description and any mapped columns (columns win): the original amount and currency (`USD 20.00`, `20.00 USD`, `20.00 دولار`), the rate (`@ 3.84`, `FX rate 3.8400`, `rate: 3.84`, `سعر الصرف 3.84`), a markup (`markup 2.5%`, `هامش 2.5%`) and a fee (`intl txn fee SAR 1.20`, `رسوم 1.20 ر.س`). A fee or markup on a row with no foreign amount or rate is not an FX detail: a row that is only a fee is its own `fees` expense.

- The posted ledger amount stays the bank-billed amount in the account's currency. The original amount, rate, fee and markup go in the transaction's notes (as before) and in the preview.
- **A rate is never invented.** It is printed (`printed`), from a mapped column (`column`) or worked out from two printed amounts (`implied`, labelled "computed from the two amounts").
- The preview checks billed against original x rate, plus the printed fee or markup when that comes closer, and shows the difference. Within two minor units of the account's currency (0.02 SAR, 0.002 KWD) it ties. When the rate is printed it also shows the markup the billed amount implies, labelled as computed.
- A fee printed inside the purchase row is informational; a separate fee row is its own `fees` expense (unchanged).

### Card FX and fees

A card or bank row with an original foreign amount and a billed amount (from the statement's columns or words, or the bank SMS) is read by `finance/card_fx.py` (pure) and shown in the transaction drawer, **Currency and bank fee** (`GET /transactions/{id}/fx`, web `FinanceFxDetails.tsx`, `lib/financeFx.ts`): the original amount and currency, the billed amount, the rate with its date (the day the bank posted the row) and its source (statement, column, bank message or entered), and the bank's FX fee or markup with where it comes from. Code: `finance/card_fx.py`, `finance/card_fx_api.py`; contracts `FinanceTransactionFx` in `packages/contracts/src/financeRegister.ts`; tests `services/api/tests/test_finance_card_fx.py`, web `test/financeFx.test.ts`.

| Rate | When |
|---|---|
| `printed` | The bank printed it on the row or in its message |
| `column` | A mapped statement column held it |
| `implied` | Worked out from the two printed amounts (the purchase without an included fee, over the original), labelled so |
| `not_stated` | Only the billed amount exists (no original amount): the drawer says "rate not stated". **A rate is never guessed** |

**The purchase is never inflated by the bank's charge.** The fee is one of:

| Source | What happens |
|---|---|
| `fee_row` | A separate statement row on the same account and currency, money out, 0 to 3 days after the purchase, with FX or international fee words, and an amount that fits (its own stated markup on the purchase within two minor units, else at most 10 %). One candidate only. **Link the fee row** (owner) files it as type `bank_fee`, category `fees`, pointing at its purchase (`related_transaction_id`); no amount changes |
| `markup_stated` | A markup percentage stated on the purchase row (inline): the billed amount includes it, so the purchase is billed / (1 + markup). **Separate the fee** (owner, with the figures shown and the `plan_hash` of the view) corrects the purchase to the billed amount less the fee through the audited correction path (its journal entry is reversed and reposted in the same canonical chain) and records the fee as its own `bank_fee` line on the same account, day and statement (`related_transaction_id` the purchase, statement-confirmed), so the account's balance and the statement's balance check do not change. The purchase's notes say `FX fee separated: <fee> <currency>`, so re-reading it shows the split as done |
| `fee_stated` | A fee amount stated on the purchase row with a printed rate, when billed = original x rate + fee ties within two minor units: as `markup_stated` |

A fee stated on a row without a printed rate that shows it is part of the billed amount is shown (`fee_not_provably_included`) and never split. Rows recorded from an iPhone SMS or Apple Pay are read here and corrected only under Finance, iPhone SMS, Review (`can_correct: false`). Nothing changes without the owner: the queue lists separable fees as `fx_fee` suggestions whose Review opens the transaction ([Suggestions](#suggestions)). The phone shows the same section on a transaction's page (tap a row in Transactions, or Review on an `fx_fee` suggestion), with Separate the fee and Link the fee row behind a confirm with the figures and the `plan_hash`, and "rate not stated" when only the billed amount exists.

**Own transfers in two currencies.** For an own transfer pair ([Own transfers](#own-transfers)) whose sides are in two currencies (AED out of one account, SAR into another), the drawer shows what was sent and received and the **realised rate** (received / sent). When the person's exchange rates (`finance_fx_rates`, published or typed) hold a rate on or before the transfer day within the plan's maximum age (`finance/fx.py`, direct, inverse or cross), the money sent is valued at it and the **realised FX difference** (received less that value, in the received currency) is its own line, a gain or a loss. **Policy:** the difference is worked out on read and never posted; it counts as neither spending nor income (both sides stay an internal transfer). With no earlier rate there is no difference, only the realised rate.

### Bills

A row that pays one of the person's saved, active bills is recorded as that due date's payment through the same path the Bills page uses (`links.create`, dimension `bill`, docs: [Bills and budgets](#bills-and-budgets)); the due date's status is then `paid`. A due date absorbs at most one row, and a due date already matched is not offered.

| Strength | Rule | On import |
|---|---|---|
| Strong | The bill's name shares a meaningful word with the row, the amount is within 10 % of the bill's expected amount, and the row is from 7 days before to 10 days after the due date | Recorded as paid (untick to skip) |
| Weak | The name agrees but the amount or the date does not (within 30 days) | A link waiting for review (`needs_review`, reason `weak_match`): the due date shows `review`, never `paid`, until the person confirms or removes it under Bills |

Without the bill's name in the row there is no bill match at all, whatever the amount.

### Own transfers

Money out of one of the person's accounts and the same money into another of them is one transfer, not an expense and an income (for example 1000 leaving the salary account and 1000 arriving on the card ending 5377).

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Accounts | Two different accounts of the same person in the same workspace; never two rows on one account |
| Amount | Equal and opposite; in different currencies, one side must state the other side's amount (an original amount) |
| Date | The money in arrives 0 to 5 days after the money out |
| Strong | Plus a transfer or card payment word in either text (`transfer`, `TRF`, `card payment`, `payment received`, `تحويل`, `سداد`), or the other account's last four digits or its own name in the text |
| Weak | Amount and date only: listed under "Needs your decision", paired only when ticked |

The pairing runs against rows already in the ledger too (the other side may have been imported earlier or come from an SMS), one to one, and skips records already paired. On import both sides become category `transfer` (out of spend and income in every summary, as before) where they are hand-entered or statement records; an SMS or Apple Pay side keeps its reading (an SMS transfer is already a transfer) and is corrected under Review if it is not. The pair is kept as a finance link in the new `transfer` dimension: the money-out side's canonical transaction links to the money-in side's (`target_type: finance_transfer`), once (`single_payment`), checked by `commitments.vet_transfer` (money out, two different accounts). **Unpair.** The transaction drawer (web) and a transaction's page (phone) show the pair with the other side's account, day, amount and text (`GET /api/v1/finance/links/transfer?transaction_id=`, `finance:read`, the caller's own ledger: `{paired, link_id, link_version, role: out|in, other}`), and an **Unpair** button that, after a confirmation, voids the pair's link through the links API (`POST /api/v1/finance/links/{id}/void {version}`, `finance:manage`; a stale version is refused). Both rows stay with the categories they have now (change one yourself if a side is spending or income), and the pair is offered again under Find transfers. Tests: `services/api/tests/test_finance_transfer_unpair.py`, `test_postgres_finance_split_unpair.py`, `apps/web/e2e/finance-evidence.spec.ts`, `apps/mobile/test/finance_depth_test.dart`.

**Find transfers** pairs rows that are already in the ledger: `GET /api/v1/finance/transfers/candidates?start&end` (a preview, at most 400 days, nothing changes) and `POST /api/v1/finance/transfers {start, end, pairs: [{out_record_id, into_record_id}]}` (only the ticked pairs; each is checked again and refused with `not_available` or `not_a_transfer` if it changed). Both need `finance:manage` and read only the caller's own ledger. Finance, Import shows it under "Transfers between your accounts"; nothing pairs on its own.

### Reconciliation

The preview checks the statement against its own balances: the opening balance plus every row (new, already recorded and matched alike) against the closing balance. The balances come from the person (opening and closing fields, as printed on the statement), from a mapped running balance column (CSV: `Balance`, `Running balance`, `الرصيد`; a newest-first export is put in date order first) or from the PDF reader. The answer is `tied`, `differs` (with the difference and the rows after which the running balance breaks) or `unknown` (nothing to check against). It is stored on the import (`finance_imports.reconciliation`, migration `0145_finance_statement_match`) with `not_in_ledger`: how many of the statement's rows the ledger does not hold after the import (held for review, or left out by the person).

**Balances that tie are not enough.** Once an account has a completed [reconciliation session](#reconciliation-sessions), `last_reconciled_at` is that completion. Before that, `GET /accounts/overview` counts a statement as the account's last reconciliation (`last_reconciled_at`) only when it ties **and** every one of its rows is in the ledger (imported, attached to the record it confirms, or already recorded). A tied statement with rows held or left out says "Last statement balances (file), N rows not in the ledger" on the account's card and adds `statement_rows_missing` to what needs attention; `last_statement.not_in_ledger` gives N, and the rows are listed on the import (`GET /imports/{id}?disposition=not_in_ledger`, [Import history](#import-history)). A statement that does not tie adds `statement_differs`. Rows the reader could not use (an unreadable CSV line such as a footer, a PDF row outside the statement period) are listed on the import but do not block reconciliation: they are not part of the balance check. Imports made before migration `0149_finance_import_rows` have no `not_in_ledger` and are read as before.

### Import history

Every import stores what happened to each row of the statement (`finance_imports.dispositions`, migration `0149_finance_import_rows`, expand only) and the counts:

| Disposition | Meaning | Counted in |
|---|---|---|
| `imported` | Added as a new transaction (`record_id`: the transaction) | `imported_count` |
| `attached_to_existing` | Confirmed an SMS, Apple Pay or hand-entered record: no second transaction (`record_id`: that record; `reason`: the match code) | `reconciled` (`attached_count`) |
| `duplicate_skipped` | Already recorded (`reason`: `imported_before`, `reconciled_before`, `same_statement_row`, or `already_in_ledger` when another import stored the same row at the same moment) | `duplicate_count` |
| `held_for_review` | Waits in Finance, iPhone SMS, Review (`reason`: `two_candidates`, `amount_date_only`, or `waiting_review` when an earlier import already holds it) | `held_for_review` (`held_count`) |
| `unticked` | Left out by the person (unticked, or "skip") | `skipped` (`skipped_count`) |
| `dropped_by_reader` | Not read: an unreadable CSV line (`unreadable`, with the reader's message) or a PDF row outside the statement period or a line no row could take ([rows left out](finance-statements.md#rows-left-out)) | `dropped` (`dropped_count`) |

`duplicate_count` no longer counts attached rows (it did before; attached rows are `reconciled`). `GET /api/v1/finance/imports` answers the stored counts for every import (they were 0 in the list before) plus `not_in_ledger`; `GET /api/v1/finance/imports/{id}?disposition=&limit=&offset=` lists the rows, filtered by disposition or `not_in_ledger`, at most 500 a page. **Where the owner finds them:** Finance, Connections, Import history shows per import Added, Duplicates, Matched, For review and Not in ledger, and **Rows** opens the list (it starts on "Not in the ledger" when there are any); held rows are confirmed or dismissed in Finance, iPhone SMS, Review (the list's **Open Review** button). Finance, Import's own history line adds the matched, held and left out counts and the rows not in the ledger. Re-importing the same statement adds nothing new: rows already recorded are `duplicate_skipped`, rows already waiting in Review stay held once.

- **Rows the person leaves out are not matched.** A row ticked off (CSV `skip`, PDF unticked) is kept out of the matching engine, so it never takes an SMS record another row of the statement needs.
- **Identical rows keep their place.** Two identical purchases on one day are told apart by their occurrence number in the statement. The PDF import now receives unticked rows too (`include: false`, never checked or imported), so a row keeps the occurrence number it had in the preview when another identical row is unticked; and a record imported from one row of a statement is never taken for another identical row of the same statement (`file_hashes`), so importing the second one later still adds it.
- **No journal entry without a row.** Rows that `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` leaves out (another import stored them a moment earlier) get no journal entry and no links (`insert_transactions` answers the ids actually inserted).

### Undoing an import

`DELETE /api/v1/finance/imports/{id}` (`finance:manage`, 204; 404 for an import that is gone or not yours) undoes the whole import in one unit of work under the owner's lock (`statement_ledger.detach_import`):

- the transactions it added are reversed in the journal and deleted;
- every row it **attached** to a recorded payment is detached, whether it confirmed one payment (`attached_to_existing`) or several (`split_match`): its statement source is dismissed (`import_undone`) and the payment loses its reconciled mark unless a row of another import still confirms it, so the next import of that statement matches it again;
- rows it **held** in Review leave Review (`import_undone`), since the statement they came from is undone.

A second undo finds nothing (404) and a repeated detach changes nothing. An amount the import corrected to the bank-billed figure keeps the corrected amount (that is what the bank charged; change it under Transactions if needed). Audit: `finance.import.delete` with `removed` and `detached` counts. Tests: `services/api/tests/test_finance_statement_split.py` (single row, split group, another import still confirming, repeat), `test_postgres_finance_split_unpair.py`.

## Reconciliation sessions

Finance, Reconciliation (`#finance-reconciliation`, under data capture in the sidebar) checks one account's statement for one period against the books at the same dates, per bank or card account (cash and loan accounts may be reconciled too; investment accounts are checked under Holdings). Owner only: sessions and resolutions are the person's own (forced RLS, `owner_only_*`). Code: `finance/reconciliation.py` (the figures and the exceptions, pure, plus the reader), `finance/reconciliation_api.py` (routes), `finance/reconciliation_store.py` and `db/reconciliation_repository.py` (migration `0153_finance_reconciliation`); contracts `packages/contracts/src/financeReconciliation.ts`; web `apps/web/src/FinanceReconciliation.tsx`, `lib/financeReconciliation.ts`, `lib/financeReconciliationApi.ts`, `lib/messages/financeRecon.{en,ar}.ts`, `financeRecon.css`.

**Starting one.** From an imported statement (`import_id`: its period and its opening and closing balances; an import now stores the period it covers in `finance_imports.reconciliation`, the printed period of a PDF or the first and last row date) or from the balances the owner types as printed. Balances are in the ledger's sign: a card or loan that owes money is negative (the web asks "owed" and sends it negative). A card statement also takes the amount due, the minimum payment and the due date. One session per account and exact period (409 `period_taken`); the period ends no later than today and spans at most 400 days.

**The equation, never forced.**

| Figure | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Book opening | The account's opening balance plus every ledger row before the period |
| Money in, money out | The period's rows |
| Book closing | Opening plus the period's rows |
| Marked not on this statement | Rows the owner marked `pending`: in the books, not yet on the bank's statement |
| Adjusted book closing | Book closing less the rows marked pending |
| Unexplained difference | Statement closing less the adjusted book closing |

A session is `balanced` when the unexplained difference is 0 and no statement row is missing from the books; otherwise `unresolved`, with the blockers `difference` and `statement_rows_not_in_ledger`. There is no plug or balancing entry anywhere: completing an unresolved session is refused (409 `unresolved`), and a correcting entry exists only for a real statement row, at that row's own date and amount.

**Exceptions** (each with a severity: `blocking`, `warning` or `info`, and the actions that settle it):

| Kind | When | Actions |
|---|---|---|
| `not_in_ledger` | A row of the account's imports dated in the period that the books do not hold, from the import's [dispositions](#import-history): held for review or unticked (blocking), left out by the reader (warning), skipped as a duplicate (information). A held row that Review has since confirmed, attached or dismissed is gone | match, post a correcting entry |
| `unmatched_payment`, `unmatched_deposit` | A row of the period no statement confirmed (not imported from one, not attached to one) | on this statement (attach), not on this statement (pending), match |
| `pending` | An SMS or Apple Pay record no statement confirmed; a warning once older than 10 days | attach, pending, match |
| `possible_duplicate` | Same amount within 3 days and a shared merchant word ([merchant matching](#how-matching-works)); a duplicate SMS and the statement row that confirmed it are one record and never listed | open Transactions |
| `wrong_direction` | Rent shown as money in, an income, refund or cashback type paid out, or a spending type paid in | open Transactions |
| `reversal_pair` | A debit and an equal credit within 7 days with reversal words (`reversal`, `void`, `chargeback`, `cancelled`, `عكس`, `إلغاء`) or the same bank reference. A suggestion | accept as reversal |
| `unknown_currency` | A row in another currency than the account's, or an original currency that is not an ISO code | open Transactions |
| `unrecognized_fee` | Money out with fee words (`fee`, `commission`, `markup`, `service charge`, `رسوم`, `عمولة`) whose type is not bank fee, interest or tax | open Transactions |
| `missing_period` | A month in the period with activity on a day no imported statement covers, or a whole month inside a gap between two statement periods | import |
| `opening_differs` | The statement's opening balance is not the book opening | check the previous period |
| `amount_due_differs` | A card's amount due is not the card's liability in the books at the period's end | |

The card check shows the amount due, what the books say is owed (minus the closing), the difference, the minimum payment, the money paid into the card after the period (up to the due date) and whether that covers the minimum.

**Resolutions** are the owner's, one at a time, under the owner's lock: `match` a statement row to a ledger record (equal amounts only), `attach` (this record is on the statement), `pending` (not on this statement: it leaves the adjusted book closing), `reversal` (accept a debit and its equal credit as a pair; the types are not changed, set `reversal` under Transactions if wanted) and `correction`. A statement row and a ledger record have at most one live resolution per session: the store refuses a second (409 `already_resolved`), and in PostgreSQL partial unique indexes enforce it, so two requests resolving the same row at the same moment cannot both win. Any resolution but a correction can be undone while the session is open.

**Correcting entry.** `POST /reconciliations/{id}/corrections/preview {statement_ref, category?}` shows the transaction and its journal legs (account and category, balanced) with a payload hash; `POST /reconciliations/{id}/corrections {statement_ref, payload_hash}` posts exactly that (409 `payload_changed` if anything moved) through the hand-entry path: one transaction and one balanced journal entry, with a deterministic id so a retried approval posts once. Audit `finance.reconciliation.correct` with ids only. Change or delete a posted correction under Transactions.

**Completing and reopening.** `POST /reconciliations/{id}/complete {version}` records who, when, the figures, the counts, the confirmed rows and the resolutions (`snapshot`) and a fingerprint of every ledger row of the account up to the period's end and its opening balance. A later change that moves the book closing (a row added, edited or deleted up to that date) makes the session `changed`: it shows "Changed since completed", the account card adds `reconciliation_changed` to what needs attention, and resolving needs a reopen first. `POST /reconciliations/{id}/reopen {version, reason}` keeps the completion in the `history` with the reason. Both compare and set on `version` (409 `stale`). A session that was ever completed cannot be deleted.

**Where "reconciled" comes from.** The register's `reconciled` is now "covered by a completed, unchanged reconciliation of its account" (`reconciliation_id`, rows marked pending excluded) once the account has one; before that it keeps its older meaning, a statement confirmed the record, which stays available as `statement_confirmed` (the totals' `matched` counts it). The account card's `last_reconciled_at` is the latest completed, unchanged session; `reconciliation` gives the latest session's id, status (`open`, `completed`, `changed`) and period.

**Connection health** (`GET /reconciliations/health`, the page's lower card): per account the last statement period covered, the last import, the last SMS and Apple Pay record, months with activity no statement covers (bank and card accounts, before the current month), gaps between statement periods, the date it is reconciled through and the next step in plain words (`import_statement` with the month, `reconcile`, `finish_reconciliation`, `review_changes`, `up_to_date`). `bank_feed` is always `not_connected`: ANUM has no live bank connection and never says otherwise. The ECB rates connector keeps its own health under [Connectors](#connectors).

**For the review queue and the page's exceptions table.** `GET /reconciliations/exceptions?account_id=&sort=&desc=&limit=&offset=` (Python: `finance.reconciliation_api.list_open_exceptions(context, account_id=None, limit=50, offset=0, sort=None, desc=False)`) lists every blocking and warning exception of each account's open or changed session, then the missing months of accounts with no open session, each with its session, account (id and name) and period. `sort` orders by `severity` (what blocks first), `account`, `period`, `kind`, `posted` or `amount` (any other value: 422); without it the queue keeps its own order. Read only: an exception is settled on its session. `GET /reconciliations/exceptions/export?account_id=&sort=&desc=` is the same list as a CSV file (`anum-reconciliation-exceptions.csv`, up to 20000 rows; formula-safe cells; the statement's own words, no account numbers; audit `finance.reconciliation.exceptions_export` with the row count). The [suggestion queue](#suggestions) lists them as `reconciliation` items whose Review opens the session. On the Reconciliation page the card "Everything that needs you" shows this list above the sessions: sortable headers (`aria-sort`, the order goes to the server), 20 a page, Export CSV, and per row its next step: Open reconciliation (its session), Import statement (a missing month) or Open transactions.

**Editing and deleting a session (web).** An open session's head has Edit figures (`PATCH /reconciliations/{id}` with the `version` the page saw: the statement's opening and closing balances as printed, owed figures typed positive for a card or loan, and for a card the amount due, minimum payment and due date; the equation is worked out again, the books are not touched) and Delete (`DELETE /reconciliations/{id}` after a confirmation that names the account and period). A completed session shows neither and says it keeps its history; a reopened one offers Delete and shows the server's refusal (409 `has_history`) in its own words, like `stale` and `completed` on an edit.

Every route: reading `finance:read`, every change `finance:manage`; audit rows (`finance.reconciliation.create`, `.update`, `.delete`, `.resolve`, `.undo`, `.correct`, `.complete`, `.reopen`) hold ids and codes only. Deleting an account deletes its sessions; "Delete my data" and the export include them.

**Not built:** reconciling against a live bank feed (none exists); pairing one statement row with several ledger rows; reading a card statement's minimum payment from the PDF facts into a session (typed for now); resolving an exception (match, attach, pending, reversal, a correcting entry), starting, completing and reopening a session on the phone, which stay on the web ([Pending work](pending-work.md#build-work-still-open-with-reasons)).

**On the phone** (Finance, More in Finance, Reconciliation, or Open reconciliation on the Connections card; `apps/mobile/lib/features/finance/finance_recon_screens.dart`): "Everything that needs you" lists the open exceptions across accounts with the same server-side sort (What blocks first, Severity, Account, Period, Kind, Date, Amount, ascending or descending), 20 a page, and **Export CSV** through the system save dialog ([Exports on the phone](mobile.md#exports)); a row with a session opens it. The sessions list filters by All, Open, Completed, Changed and Unresolved. A session shows its state, the equation, the card check and what blocks completing it, its exceptions 50 at a time, and while it is open **Edit figures** (`PATCH` with the `version` read: the statement's opening and closing as printed, owed figures typed positive for a card or loan and sent negative, and for a card the amount due, minimum and due date) and **Delete** after a confirmation that names the account and period; `stale`, `completed` and `has_history` are shown in the API's words. **Start a reconciliation** picks the account (archived and investment accounts are not offered), then an imported statement of that account or the period and balances typed as printed (owed figures positive for a card or loan, sent negative; for a card the amount due, minimum and due date), and opens the new session (`POST /reconciliations`). While a session is open each exception offers the actions the API lists for it: **Match** (a sheet with only the session's records, or statement rows, of the same amount, the web's `matchCandidates`), **On this statement** and **Not on this statement** (`attach`, `pending`), **Accept as reversal** (`reversal` with both records), **Post correcting entry** (the preview's journal lines and the first characters of its payload hash, then **Approve and post** sends exactly that hash; a changed entry is refused in the API's words, `payload_changed`), and **Open transactions** or **Import statement**, which close the session for Finance, Transactions or Import a statement. **Your decisions** lists the resolutions with **Undo** (a posted correcting entry is changed under Transactions instead); **Complete reconciliation** sends the version read and is enabled only when the session can complete, with the reason under it otherwise; a completed or changed session has **Reopen** with a reason (at least 3 characters), and **History** lists every completion and reopening with its reason. Code: `finance_recon_screens.dart`, `finance_recon_actions.dart`. Tests: `apps/mobile/test/finance/finance_more_test.dart`, `test/finance/finance_tools_test.dart` and the journey `integration_test/journeys/finance.dart`.

## Categories

Rule-based only, no AI and no network (`finance/categories.py`): the person's own rules first (a phrase matched as a whole word or phrase, so "gym" never matches "GYMNASTICS"; an income, cashback or refunds rule never files money out), then built-in keyword rules for common UAE and Saudi merchants and statement wording (Carrefour, Lulu, Spinneys, Panda: groceries; DEWA, SEWA, ADDC: utilities; Etisalat, du: phone and internet; Salik, RTA, Careem: transport; ENOC, ADNOC: fuel; salary and WPS: income; interest; transfers; ATM; fees), matched as whole words. **Anything else is uncategorized**: category `other`, [type](#transaction-types) `uncategorized`, held for review and counted as neither income nor spend (the owner's rule of November 2026: a credit is not automatically income, a debit is not automatically spending). A category or type picked by hand is never changed by a rule or a match.

Order matters, and these win over the generic words:

- **Card payments** ("credit card payment", "CC payment", "card repayment", "autopay", "payment received, thank you", "سداد بطاقة الائتمان", "تسديد البطاقة") are type `card_payment`, category `transfer`.
- **Money in on a card or loan account** is the payment from the person's own bank: a card payment, unless a rule files it as cashback, a refund, interest or a merchant's credit (a merchant's credit on a card, "CARREFOUR" +45, is a refund in groceries).
- **Salary and WPS** win over "transfer" ("WPS SALARY TRANSFER" is income), and a **specific spending word** (rent, room rent, landlord, school fees, tuition) wins over "transfer" (rent paid by transfer is housing or room rent spending).
- **Interest**: money in with interest or savings-profit words ("interest earned", "INTEREST", "profit credit") is income under category `interest`; money out with them ("LATE PAYMENT INTEREST", "finance charge") is type `interest` (spending) under category `interest`, never a bank fee. Fees ("annual fee", "late fee") stay `fees`, type `bank_fee`.
- **Transfers**: words that name the person's own accounts ("own account", "between your accounts", "إلى حسابك") are an `internal_transfer`. Generic transfer words ("transfer", "TRF", "IPP", "AANI", "remittance", "تحويل", "حوالة") on **money out** are a transfer to someone else. With words that say what it paid for ("rent", "إيجار") it is spending in that category; with none it is `uncategorized` (reason `transfer_out_third_party`) and not counted, because a transfer to a person may be a loan, money sent home or a payment, until the person files it or a [match](#own-transfers) pairs it with an own account. On **money in** they are `uncategorized` (`transfer_in_unknown`): money from someone else is not income until the person says what it was.

## Transaction types

Every transaction has a **type** (what it is) beside its **category** (what it was for). The type decides how it counts (`finance/txn_types.py`, `flows.flow_for_type`); the category is what spending by category groups by. The API returns `transaction_type`, `type_source` (`rule`, `owner`, `system`, `sms`, `match`), `type_confidence` (0 to 1) and `type_reason` (a short machine code such as `no_rule_matched`, `transfer_out_third_party`, `paired_own_transfer`, `refund_matched`) on every transaction and register row.

| Type | Counts as |
|---|---|
| `income` | Income (a negative row, a salary reversal, lowers it). Interest received is `income` with category `interest` |
| `expense`, `bank_fee`, `interest` (charged), `tax` | Spending in the row's category |
| `refund`, `reversal` | Lowers the spending of its category when that is a spending category; under `refunds` (no purchase found) its own line; a reversal filed as income lowers income |
| `cashback` | Its own line |
| `internal_transfer`, `card_payment`, `loan_received`, `loan_principal`, `investment_contribution`, `investment_withdrawal`, `owner_contribution`, `owner_drawing`, `reimbursement`, `receivable_collection`, `payable_settlement`, `fx_exchange` | Neither income nor spending: money the person already has or owes moves |
| `uncategorized` | Neither, held for review: the register shows it with status `uncategorized` and its reason, and the summary and ledger dashboard say "N uncategorized, not counted" (`uncategorized_count`, with the amounts in and out per currency) instead of a silent zero |

Where a type comes from:

- **Rules** (`rule`): the [category rules](#categories) above, for statements, CSV and PDF imports, hand-entered rows without a category and synced rows. Built-in matches carry 0.8 to 0.9, the generic transfer reading 0.6 and an unrecognised row 0.
- **The owner** (`owner`, confidence 1): a category or a type chosen on Transactions (`PATCH /transactions/{id}` with `category` or `transaction_type`; a chosen type with no category files the row under that type's category, for example `income` under income). The owner's choice is never changed by a rule, a transfer pair or a refund match (audit C4).
- **The message** (`sms`): an iPhone SMS or Apple Pay kind ([iPhone SMS kinds](finance-sms.md#kinds)): salary is income; a purchase or a transfer to someone else is spending filed by the merchant's words (rent is housing, as from a statement); an own transfer, card repayment, loan disbursement and loan repayment are transfers (`internal_transfer`, `card_payment`, `loan_received`, `loan_principal`) and the loan interest the message states is `interest`; a fee is `bank_fee`; a refund `refund` (or `cashback`); a deposit or a transfer received from someone is `uncategorized` until the person files it.
- **A match** (`match`): a strong own-transfer pair at import files both plain sides as `internal_transfer` (or `card_payment`) with category `transfer` and source `match`; a pair the person ticks is their own choice (`manual`, `owner`). A refund that finds its purchase (below) is `match` too.

**Rows stored before types existed** (no stored type) are read with the type their category, sign and account kind stand for (`txn_types.derive`, reason `legacy_category`), the same answer for every reader; it is stored the next time the row is saved. This is the backfill: a migration cannot update the forced-RLS table without a tenant context, and the rule is deterministic. Only the two cases the November 2026 audit found wrong change: an `income` row no rule matched (`category_source: none`) and an `other` row no rule matched are `uncategorized`.

Loans have one policy: a repayment moves the whole payment from the bank to the loan (`loan_principal` on both sides, a transfer), and the interest is charged on the loan as its own row (type `interest`, category `interest`), so only the interest is spending; a payment whose split the message or statement does not state stays one `loan_principal` transfer until the person enters the interest. The debt estimate (`obligations.py`) takes each matched payment's interest out by the debt's rate.

### Refunds and purchases

A statement, CSV, PDF or hand-entered refund with no purchase yet (category `refunds`) looks for the person's own purchase it gives back (`finance/refunds.py`): money out in the same currency within 120 days before it whose merchant words agree (`statement_match.merchant_words`, without the words "refund", "reversal" and the like). One purchase of the same amount is a **match**: the refund is filed under the purchase's category, so it lowers that category's spending, and `related_transaction_id` names the purchase (`refund_matched`, source `match`). Anything less certain (a partial refund, two purchases that fit) is only a **suggestion**: the refund stays on its own line and `related_transaction_id` names the likeliest purchase with a low confidence (`refund_suggested`). A refund the owner filed is never touched. SMS and Apple Pay refunds keep their own merchant match ([iPhone SMS](finance-sms.md#kinds)).

## Rules and learning

The owner's rules replace the plain phrase list (`FinanceSettings.category_rules`), which is still read until the first rule is saved and is then copied in once as approved `imported` rules (the old list is left as it was, so the previous release keeps working). Code: `finance/rules.py` (pure), `rules_store.py`, `rules_service.py`, routes `rules_api.py`; tables from migration `0152_finance_rules_learning`.

The engine runs in a fixed order for every new row (statement, CSV, PDF, sync, hand-entered, SMS spending words):

1. **The owner's approved rules** (first match by priority, then the most specific). They win over the built-ins, as the phrase rules always did.
2. **The built-in rules** ([Categories](#categories)).
3. **Historical reuse**: the category the owner chose by hand before for the same merchant, offered on the [Suggestions](#suggestions) page with a confidence (2 of 2 choices is 0.85, 5 of 5 is 0.95, a split history lower, under 0.5 nothing). It is never applied unless an [automation policy](#automation-policies) the owner turned on says so. History learns only from rows the owner categorised by hand (`category_source: manual`), never from a rule's guess, a match, an automatic change or an AI answer.
4. **An AI suggestion**, only on request in the transaction drawer (`POST /transactions/{id}/ai-suggestion`), only with AI help turned on (the same consent as [iPhone SMS AI help](finance-sms.md#ai-help)) and a real model. The model sees the masked description; its answer is shown, never applied, never stored and never learned from (a row the owner files from it carries `type_reason: owner_ai`, which history skips). No unit of work is open while the model is asked.

A **rule** matches on any of these, all of which must hold: the merchant (after the owner's [aliases](#merchants-and-aliases)), description words matched as whole words, a reference written as plain text (never a regular expression), money in or out, an amount range (with its currency; on the absolute amount), the accounts it is limited to, and the bank or SMS sender. A rule needs a merchant, words or a reference, and words that are only common banking words ("payment", "transfer", "rent") are refused as too broad. A rule gives a category, an optional [subcategory](#subcategories) under it, and an optional [type](#transaction-types) (else the type the category stands for). An income, cashback or refunds rule never files money out. A rule never changes an amount, a currency or a date, and no rule, policy or suggestion changes a row whose category or type the owner chose by hand.

Every change is a **version** (`finance_rules`, one row per version, never overwritten). A version is first `proposed`, with a payload hash over exactly what it says; it runs only once approved with that hash (the Guardian's binding rule). Editing proposes the next version while the approved one keeps running; reverting proposes a new version with an older version's content (`reverted_from`). Each version keeps its provenance (`owner`, `learned` from a correction with the transaction id, `imported` from the old list, or `agent`), confidence, who proposed and who decided, and when. An assistant can propose a rule (`finance.rules.propose`) but never approve one.

**Learning from a correction.** When the owner changes a row's category or type in the Transactions drawer, **Apply to similar** asks `POST /transactions/{id}/similar` (`scope`: `all` or `account`) for the rule the correction suggests (the merchant, the same side, the chosen category, subcategory and type) and a preview of which other rows it would change. Nothing is saved until the owner saves the rule, approves it and, as a separate step, applies it to earlier rows.

**Applying a rule to earlier rows** is an explicit owner action: `POST /rules/{id}/preview` lists the rows the approved version would change (and counts those it leaves: already filed that way, set by hand, or recorded from an SMS or Apple Pay message, which are corrected under SMS review so the journal records it), and `POST /rules/{id}/apply` with the preview's hash applies exactly that, worked out again under the owner's lock (a changed preview is refused). The change is one **batch** (`finance_rule_batches`) holding every row's values from before; a plain row's journal entry is reposted. `POST /rule-batches/{id}/undo` puts every row back unless it changed since (then it is left and counted as kept). Applies and undos are audited with ids and counts only.

The older **Remember this category** tick in the drawer still works: before the first versioned rule it adds to the phrase list as before; afterwards it saves an approved `learned` rule.

### Merchants and aliases

A description's merchant is its first three meaningful words, without card numbers, dates, channel words ("POS", "Apple Pay", "شراء عبر نقاط البيع"), cities, country codes and legal forms (`rules.merchant_key`; "POS 1234 CARREFOUR CITY CENTRE DUBAI AE" is "carrefour city centre"). Arabic letter forms are folded first (أ إ آ to ا, ة to ه, ى to ي, no diacritics), so a rule or alias typed either way matches both. The owner can name several spellings, English or Arabic, as one merchant (`GET, POST /merchants/aliases`, `PATCH, DELETE /merchants/aliases/{id}`; at most 500): a rule on the merchant "Zebra Mart" then matches "شراء زيبرا" and "POS ZEBRA MRT" alike, and history counts them together.

### Subcategories

The owner can add subcategories under a built-in category (`GET, POST /subcategories`, `PATCH /subcategories/{id}` to rename or archive; at most 200, unique name per category). A row's `subcategory_id` is optional and must belong to the row's category; choosing one is the owner's choice. Every existing summary keeps grouping by the category, so nothing changes for rows without one; `GET /subcategories/spend?month=` splits each category's spending by subcategory per currency (the same totals).

## Suggestions

One queue for everything that waits for the owner (`finance/suggestions.py`, `GET /suggestions?kind=&limit=&offset=`, web Finance, Suggestions). Each suggestion says what was detected, why, the evidence (the rows, import, message, bill or account it rests on), a confidence where there is one, the money it is about (per currency, never added across currencies), the proposed action and the page that owns it. Kinds, in the order the page lists them:

| Kind | From | Accept |
|---|---|---|
| `cash_shortage` | [Safe to spend](#safe-to-spend) below zero in a currency, or the [forecast](#forecast) going below zero or below the emergency reserve (`params.reason`, with the date) | Acknowledge |
| `bill_due` | A bill due in the next 7 days with no matched payment | Acknowledge |
| `unusual_spend` | Spending at least 3 times the usual payment to that merchant (3 or more earlier payments) | Acknowledge |
| `reconciliation` | An open exception (blocking or warning) of a [reconciliation session](#reconciliation-sessions), or a month with activity and no statement on an account without an open session (left out when `missing_statement` already lists that month) | Review opens the session |
| `duplicate` | Two records that look like the same payment (the register's rule) | Open Transactions |
| `conflict`, `classification` | Historical reuse disagrees with a rule, or fills an uncategorized or uncertain row | Files the row (one batch, undoable) |
| `uncategorized` | No rule recognised the row and there is no history | Open Transactions |
| `statement_hold` | Statement rows held for review ([Import history](#import-history)) | Open Import |
| `sms_review` | A bank message waiting in [SMS review](finance-sms.md) | Open iPhone SMS |
| `refund_match` | A refund whose purchase is only a suggestion ([Refunds and purchases](#refunds-and-purchases)) | Confirms the link (undoable) |
| `payment_match` | A bill due date that passed with no matched payment; when the one payment matcher (`GET /payment-candidates`) finds a payment that fits, it is proposed | Links the payment to the due date through the links API (the bill counts as paid), else Open Bills |
| `attribution` | What else a payment is: a business allocation, a vehicle cost, a purchase or a debt payment ([Attribution suggestions](#attribution-suggestions)) | Makes the link (or allocation) through the links API; Open Vehicles for a vehicle cost with no record yet |
| `loan_overdue` | A repayment of a [loan with a person](#lent-and-borrowed) whose date passed and that the linked repayments do not cover, with what is overdue in the loan's currency | Acknowledge |
| `loan_payments` | Every instalment of a [bank loan](#bank-loans) due so far: found (a bank debit, or a payslip or salary rule showing the deduction), missing or ambiguous; one suggestion per loan, "N payments found for <loan>" | Links every found bank debit (`debt` dimension) and records the salary months in one batch, undone with `POST /loan-batches/{id}/undo`; ambiguous months wait for a pick on the loan page |
| `loan_repayment` | A transaction that looks like a repayment of an open loan with a person (the right direction and currency, on or after the day the money changed hands, not linked yet, naming the person or exactly the next repayment) | Links it to the loan (`person_loan` dimension, `repayment` role) through the links API |
| `fx_fee` | A foreign card purchase whose FX fee can be separated or linked ([Card FX and fees](#card-fx-and-fees)) | Review opens the transaction |
| `missing_statement` | A month with no statement rows between months that have them, or a newest statement older than 45 days | Open Import |
| `subscription` | A steady payment not yet saved as a bill (`detect_recurring`) | Open Bills |
| `external` | Another module's producer that has no kind of its own | Acknowledge |

Accept (`POST /suggestions/accept` with the `key` and the `payload_hash` shown) and Reject (`POST /suggestions/reject`) are stored once per key (`finance_suggestion_decisions`, unique): of two accepts one wins, and a rejected suggestion stays dismissed (a different proposal for the same row is a new key). `POST /suggestions/restore` brings back a dismissed one. Accepting a classification is the owner's choice (`manual`) and is one batch that `POST /rule-batches/{id}/undo` reverses. The pages that already review these (Transactions, Import, iPhone SMS, Bills, Guardian, Safe to spend) keep working; the queue links into them.

**Reconciliation exceptions.** The `reconciliation` producer (`finance/reconciliation_suggestions.py`) lists every open exception that `reconciliation_api.list_open_exceptions` returns (the same list as `GET /reconciliations/exceptions`; `info` exceptions stay on the session page): what was found (the exception's kind), why (a blocking one stops the session from being completed, a warning is not explained yet), the evidence (the account and period, the statement row or ledger record, its date and amount) and the money it is about. Its only action is Review, which opens the session (`#finance-reconciliation?session=<id>`); Accept is refused for it, so nothing in the queue matches, attaches, marks pending or posts a correcting entry: that happens on the session, with its own payload-hash approval. Dismissing one keeps it dismissed (its key is the session and the exception); an exception in a later session is a new key.

**Producers.** `suggestions.register_producer(name, fn)` adds a source: `fn(snapshot)` gets the owner's scope and a snapshot read in one short unit of work, may open its own short unit of work, never writes, and returns suggestions with a key unique to it. A producer that fails is listed under `unavailable` and hides nothing else. The assistant reads the queue with `finance.suggestions` (read only); no tool accepts or rejects.

## Attribution suggestions

Producers of the suggestion queue (`finance/attribution.py`, kind `attribution`, plus the bills producer's `payment_match`) that propose, for a recorded money-out row of the last 60 days (spending, or not filed yet), the record it belongs to in another module, each with a confidence and the reason. Accept makes the link through `finance.links` (`create`, or `allocate` for a business), so ownership, the currency, the amount left, single payment targets and each module's vet apply; a refused link releases the decision and answers the refusal. Nothing is linked automatically: no automation policy can link (`rules.require_automatable` refuses `link`).

| Proposes | Signals | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| A business allocation (business or personal treatment, project) | The owner allocated payments to the same merchant (merchant key with the owner's aliases) to a business before: the same business, treatment and project as the last time, while the owner may still write there. The cost centre the business gave that allocation is named in the evidence; classifying the row stays the business's step in Books, Allocations | 0.5 rising with how often (to 0.9) |
| A vehicle cost | Fuel, parking, toll, car wash, tyre or service words (English and Arabic) and a fuel entry (within 3 days) or service record (within 7 days) of the vehicles module with the same amount and currency and no payment yet. A record's figure is its typed total, else the total and date of the receipt scanned for it and confirmed in the vault (`params.source: receipt`, "from its scanned receipt" on the web and the phone); then a payment that names the receipt's merchant is proposed without fuel words, and one that names another merchant never is ([Vehicles](vehicles.md#payments)). With one vehicle and no record: Open Vehicles to record it (on the phone, **Record it in Vehicles** opens the Vehicles module, `test/finance/finance_money_test.dart`) | 0.85, 0.9 with the receipt's merchant; 0.5 |
| A purchase | A purchase of the purchases module with the same total and currency within 7 days and no payment; two that fit and words that do not tell them apart give no suggestion | 0.85 with agreeing merchant words, else 0.6 |
| A debt payment | The description names one of the owner's debts, or the amount is exactly its minimum payment (loan principal rows count too) | 0.85 both, 0.7 named, 0.55 amount only |
| A bill or installment payment | The bills producer asks the one payment matcher (`commitments_api.find_candidates`, the matcher of `GET /payment-candidates`) for an overdue due date: the same amount, or words that agree within 10 %, posted 7 days before to 10 days after. Installment plans are bills, so their due dates come the same way | 0.85, 0.7, 0.55 |

| A personal trip | A payment within the trip's dates (3 days after the end for late card postings) whose words carry the trip's place words, or that is filed as travel. Only a trip with both dates is proposed; two trips that fit give no suggestion | 0.8 words; 0.6 travel |
| A personal project | The owner tagged payments to the same merchant to the project before (within its dates when it has them), or the words carry the project's words | 0.5 rising with the share (to 0.9); 0.7 words |

Trips and projects are the person's own ([Trips and projects](#trips-and-projects)); a business project still comes with its allocation. The phone's Suggestions show these items with Link (Allocate for a business) and Dismiss through the same accept and reject routes, with the payload hash shown.

## Automation policies

Off by default (`GET, PUT /automation`, `finance_automation_settings`). The owner may turn on:

- **Auto-classify** (`auto_classify`, `min_confidence` 0.8 to 1, default 0.9, `sources`: `history`, `rule`): the daily job applies history suggestions at or above the threshold, and rules the owner marked **auto-apply** to rows of the last 90 days they match. Each run is a batch (origin `policy`, source `system`, reason `auto_history` or `owner_rule`) that the owner can undo, recorded as `auto_applied` and in the audit log (actor `anum.automation`). Rows set by hand are never touched.
- **Exception notices** (`notices`, per kind): see [Exception notices](#exception-notices).
- **Unresolved notice** (`notify_unresolved`, `notify_min_items`): one in-app notice a day (kind `finance_suggestions`, never an email or a phone push) when that many suggestions wait. The day is claimed before the notice and released if it fails.

The job is part of the daily job (`python -m anum_api.voice_retention`, reported under `finance_suggestions`, counts only; `finance/suggestion_jobs.py`), finds people as the Money advisor does and works in each person's scope. **Never automated**, refused in code (`rules.require_automatable`, tested) whatever a stored policy says: posting adjustments, deleting records, changing bank mappings, approving payments, changing accounting periods and linking a bank loan's found payments (`link_loan_payments`). The policy has no switch for them, and an unknown field is refused.

## Exception notices

`finance/exception_notices.py`, run by the daily job with the automation policies (`suggestion_jobs.run_person`, counted under `finance_suggestions` as `exception_notices`). Each kind is off until the owner turns it on in Finance, Suggestions, Automation (`notices`, `notice_exception_days` 1 to 90, default 7, `notice_uncategorized_min`, default 500). A notice is in-app only (kind `finance_exceptions`, never an email or a phone push):

| Kind | Item |
|---|---|
| `reconciliation_overdue` | An open exception of a reconciliation session (blocking or warning) dated `notice_exception_days` or more ago |
| `statement_missing` | A month with activity on an account and no statement (the reconciliation health check) |
| `uncategorized_money` | Uncategorized money of the last 120 days at or above the threshold, per currency (never added across currencies) |
| `business_unposted` | A business where the person is an owner or a named accountant with source documents the journal could not post (a missing rate, an amount too small) or draft bills waiting for approval |
| `evidence_missing` | In such a business, bills and adjustments of the last 90 days above its evidence threshold with no supporting document ([Business books](business-books.md#supporting-documents-and-evidence)) |

**Claim, then notify.** Each item is claimed for the person's local day in `finance_notice_claims` (unique per person, kind, item and day, insert-if-absent, owner-only RLS) before the notice is created; one notice per kind names how many items were claimed now, and when creating it fails the claims are released, so the next run sends it. An item already noticed today is not noticed again that day; claims older than 14 days are pruned. Business workspaces are read as the person in each business's own RLS scope after their membership is checked; the notice goes to the person's own Finance workspace. **On the phone** (Finance, More in Finance, Rules and merchants; `apps/mobile/lib/features/finance/finance_rules_screens.dart`, `finance_rules_data.dart`): four tabs. **Rules** lists the rules with Running, Waiting for approval and Turned off, a search and paging, how many changes wait and how many older phrase rules still run; **New rule** and **Change** open the editor (merchant, words, reference, direction, an amount range with its currency, an account, category, subcategory, type, priority, note, on and Apply by the daily job, checked before sending), **Preview** shows which earlier rows would change (`POST /rules/preview`), and **Save** proposes a version and asks **Approve version N?** with the first characters of its payload hash; Approve sends exactly that hash, Not now leaves it waiting. A rule's page shows what runs, the waiting version with **Approve** and **Reject**, **Apply to earlier rows** (the preview, then Apply with its `preview_hash`), and the versions with **Restore** (a new version, then the same approval). **Recent changes** lists the batches with **Undo** (restored and kept counts). **Merchants** adds, changes, searches and removes spellings; **Subcategories** adds, renames, archives and restores (Show archived ones); **Automation** saves the policy (classify with a confidence of 80 to 100 and its sources, the daily notice, each exception notice with its threshold) and lists what is never automated. Tests: `apps/mobile/test/finance/finance_tools_test.dart`, `integration_test/journeys/finance.dart`.

## How figures are counted

One definition of spend, income, refunds, cashback and transfers (`finance/flows.py`), used by the summary, the ledger dashboard, the Money advisor, budgets, the Guardian's caps and every web page that reads them, so the same row is counted the same way everywhere. Bills and debts are read with the person's matched payments (`finance/obligations.py`). This follows what mature personal-finance apps do: a card payment is a transfer between two of the person's accounts, never spending ([YNAB](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/credit-card-payments-a-guide-r1_506Q1j.md)); transfers and card payments are left out of spending, income and cash flow, and paying a card lowers the cash and the debt by the same amount, so net worth does not move; only a loan payment's interest is an expense ([Monarch](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048393292), [Monarch tips](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/28953573066260-Tips-Tricks-for-Using-Monarch)); a refund goes back into the purchase's category and lowers its spending, it is not income ([Copilot](https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/5325170-refund-and-reimbursement-transactions), [Rocket Money](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/3584530-tracking-refunds), [PocketSmith](https://learn.pocketsmith.com/categorising-your-transactions/6a6X8SseDAvLKFX67jnPeQ/managing-refund-or-reimbursement-transactions-within-pocketsmith/8jrtdmUE4Y3X8gSki3Benh)).

- **Spend**: money out in a spending category, **net of the refunds filed in that category**. A category can be below zero in a month that only has a refund. Budgets never show less than zero spent.
- **The type decides** ([Transaction types](#transaction-types)): each row's type maps to one of the lines below; rows stored before types read the type their category stands for.
- **Uncategorized**: money in or out that no rule recognised is neither income nor spend. It waits for review, and every summary says how many rows it left out ("N uncategorized, not counted"). A credit is not income until something says it is.
- **Income**: money in of type `income` (salary, payroll, dividends, interest received, a credit the person filed as income). A negative `income` row (a salary reversal typed by hand) lowers it.
- **Refunds**: money in filed under a spending category lowers that category (an SMS or Apple Pay refund is filed under the purchase it gives back, found by merchant within 120 days). A refund whose purchase is not found is filed as `refunds` and shown on its own line. Neither is income.
- **Cashback**: card rewards, their own line, neither income nor spend. The month's net (cash flow) is income plus cashback plus unmatched refunds, less spend.
- **Transfers**: between the person's own accounts (own-account words, a matched pair, an SMS own transfer) they are neither spend nor income on either side. A transfer **to someone else** is spending, from a statement and from an SMS alike (rent sent to a landlord is housing; anything else `other`), until a match pairs it with an own account; money **received from someone** is uncategorized until the person files it (a client paying by transfer is then income). Rows stored as `transfer` before types existed stay transfers.
- **Lent and borrowed with people**: a transaction linked to a loan with a person ([Lent and borrowed](#lent-and-borrowed)), the money handed over or received and every repayment, is a transfer whatever its category says: lending is not spending and being repaid is not income. The rule is read at the time of reading (`flows.py`, the `lending` set from `obligations.load`) by the summary, the Money advisor, budgets, the planner and the ledger dashboard (a statement or hand-entered row's other side and an iPhone SMS entry's income or expense posting become a transfer; postings on the person's own accounts are unchanged); the stored row is not rewritten. Removing the link (or the loan) makes the row count by its category again.
- **Card payments**: a transfer on both sides. Money in on a card or loan account that a built-in rule (or nothing) filed as income is read as a card payment received, also for rows stored before this rule; a category the person chose by hand or with their own rule is kept.
- **Own transfers told by two messages** ("transferred to" from one own account, "received from" on another, same amount and currency, within 2 days) become one own transfer ([iPhone SMS](finance-sms.md#own-transfers-told-by-two-messages)).
- **Loans**: a repayment moves the whole payment from the bank to the loan (`loan_principal`, a transfer); the interest is charged on the loan as its own row (type and category `interest`, no longer `fees`), so only the interest is spending, the same on the summary and the ledger dashboard.
- **Net worth** (`summary.net_worth`, one definition the overview, the reports and the web read): account balances, where cards and loans count by their sign (what they owe is a negative balance), plus money lent to people still owed back and holdings at what they cost, less the debts that have **no account of their own** (money borrowed from people included) and what is still due on installment plans (Tamara, Tabby). A card kept both as an account and as a debt is counted once (the phone's Finance overview shows each currency's net worth with these lines and what is left out, [Mobile app features](mobile-features.md#finance-review)). An investment account with holdings counts them at their cost basis (ANUM keeps no market prices) instead of its ledger balance, so a purchase is never counted twice; a holding without a cost basis, or in another currency than its account, is left out and listed under `net_worth_excluded`, which the Reports page shows.
- **Available money** is one figure everywhere: bank and cash accounts that are not archived (the Accounts page's Available, Safe to spend and the planner), added exactly in the currency's minor unit; investments, cards and loans are not available.
- **Bank loans with terms** ([Bank loans](#bank-loans)): a debt with a start date, the amount borrowed and the term or the instalment owes what its schedule and matched payments leave (`obligations.loan_debt`), never the balance typed, in net worth, Safe to spend, the forecast, Monthly fixed costs, the advisor and the payoff plan; its minimum is the instalment and its rate the reducing rate the instalments give. A loan the employer deducts from a salary whose expected net already takes a loan deduction this month is not a payment of its own (`from_salary`: Safe to spend, the forecast and Monthly fixed costs leave it out, listed as `from_salary`), while the payoff plan still counts its instalment.
- **Debts**: a debt kept on a card or loan account (`account_id`) owes what that account's ledger says. Any other debt owes the balance the person saved, less the principal of the payments matched to it since then (with a rate, each payment's interest, balance times APR over 12, is taken out first). It is an estimate from the person's own figures. Entering a different balance (or currency or account) makes it the new starting point; editing the name, rate, minimum or due day keeps the earlier matched payments.
- **Money borrowed from a person** ([Lent and borrowed](#lent-and-borrowed)) is a debt for every reader (`obligations.borrowed_debts`): what is left to repay, no interest (qard hasan, never a guessed rate), and a minimum only once its due date falls in this month or has passed (then the whole amount left is due). It joins the payoff order, Safe to spend, Monthly fixed costs, the advisor's coming payments, the summary's debts and net worth. A loan whose person is also saved as a Debt in the same currency (the same name) is that Debt, never counted twice. **Money lent to a person** is a receivable: net worth counts what is still owed back; nothing spends it.
- **Goals** kept in one of the person's own bank, cash or investment accounts in the goal's currency have saved what that account holds; other goals keep what the person entered.
- **Bills**: a due date with an active matched payment is paid and is not taken off again anywhere (upcoming bills, Safe to spend, the advisor's coming payments, the ledger dashboard). This month's due dates that passed without a matched payment stay in as **overdue** until a payment is matched or the person moves the bill's next due date. A debt minimum that is also saved as a bill (same currency, and the same name or the same account) is counted once, as the bill.
- **One building block per figure** (`finance/commitment_math.py`): Monthly fixed costs, Safe to spend, the Money advisor and the planner take every bill and debt figure from the same functions, so one bill or debt gives one number everywhere. A bill's **monthly equivalent** is weekly x 52 / 12, monthly as is, quarterly / 3, yearly / 12; **a one-off bill has no monthly equivalent**: Monthly fixed costs lists it as not monthly and the advisor's runway counts nothing for it, while Safe to spend still takes it off on its due date (it asks what is due, not what recurs). A debt's **minimum** is its minimum payment, at most what it owes. **Money you have** (liquid) is bank and cash accounts that are not archived. Each feature keeps its own question: Safe to spend counts what is due before the month ends, Monthly fixed costs is a monthly figure, the advisor's coming payments look 30 days ahead.

Rounding is the server's one rule everywhere, also on the web: half to even, to the currency's minor unit (`lib/financeStatements.ts` `fromMilli`). Classification at read time never rewrites a stored row: older rows keep their stored category and are read by these rules.

## Summary

`GET /api/v1/finance/summary?month=YYYY-MM` (default: this month in the person's time zone), by the rules in [How figures are counted](#how-figures-are-counted):

- balances per account (opening balance plus every transaction) and per currency;
- spend by category for the month (net of refunds in the category; transfers, income, cashback and unmatched refunds excluded), per currency;
- income, cashback, unmatched refunds, spend and the net, per currency;
- bills due in the next 30 days without a matched payment, and this month's overdue ones (`overdue: true`, `days_until` below zero);
- debts owed now and minimum payments, per currency (money borrowed from a person included); `unlinked_debts` (debts with no account of their own), `net_worth`, its parts per currency (`net_worth_parts`: accounts, receivables, holdings at cost, debts, installments, net) and what was left out (`net_worth_excluded`);
- goals and how far along they are;
- `uncategorized_count`, `uncategorized_in` and `uncategorized_out`: the month's rows no rule recognised, left out of income and spend and shown as "N uncategorized, not counted";
- `month_rows_capped` when the month had more rows than one read returns (50,000): the figures are then partial.

`GET /api/v1/finance/bills/suggestions` finds recurring payments: money out with the same payee at a steady interval (weekly, monthly, quarterly or yearly) and amount (within 15 percent), three or more times, not yet saved as a bill. One payee is one group across accounts, and one missed period (a gap of about two) does not break the pattern. A saved bill with the same currency, cadence and amount (within 15 percent) counts as already saved even when its name differs from the bank's wording ("Electricity" and "DEWA BILL PAYMENT"), so the advisor never counts it twice. The person keeps the ones that are bills.

## Finance overview

`#finance` (`src/FinanceOverviewPage.tsx`) has its own page head with Settings, Import statement, the scope switch (All finance, Personal, Business), the month and, for personal figures in more than one currency, a currency choice.

People who may not read the books see Personal only, and the switch says why; the chart then reads their own six months whatever scope was chosen.

## Register and account overview

The Accounts and Transactions and ledger pages read a joined, **read-only** view of the ledger (`finance/register_api.py`, contracts `packages/contracts/src/financeRegister.ts`, web `FinanceAccountsPage.tsx` and `FinanceTransactionsPage.tsx`). Nothing here posts, links or moves money, and nothing says an account is connected: ANUM has no live bank connection, so each account shows the real sources that feed it.

- `GET /api/v1/finance/register` takes the transaction filters (`q`, `account_id`, `category`, `from`, `to`, `direction`) plus `origin` (`sms`, `apple_pay`, `statement`, `manual`), `evidence` (`linked`, `missing`), `status` and `type` (a [transaction type](#transaction-types)), and pages on the server. Each row adds its canonical transaction id, where it came from (the journal entry's sources; a statement row says CSV or PDF), whether a statement confirmed an SMS or Apple Pay record (`reconciled`), how many receipts are linked (`document` evidence links, [Finance links](finance-links.md)), links waiting for review, a **possible duplicate** signal and, when one account is chosen, the balance after the row. Status: `duplicate_candidate`, else `needs_review` (a link needs review), else `uncategorized` (type `uncategorized`: no rule recognised it, held for review, counted in neither income nor spend; `type_reason` says why), else `linked` (a receipt) or `recorded`. Totals (count, categorized, uncategorized, receipts missing for spending and uncategorized money out, matched, money in and out per currency) cover every match, not just the page; `balances` give the beginning and ending balance per currency for the period; `review_count` is the SMS review queue. At most 10,000 matching rows are read (`truncated` says so).
- **Possible duplicate**: the same account, amount and currency within three days (`SAME_PAYMENT_DAYS`), descriptions sharing a meaningful merchant word ([how merchants agree](#how-matching-works)), not already one canonical transaction and not two lines of one statement. It is a signal only; the person checks it and deletes the wrong copy. A "not a duplicate" decision is not stored yet, so a pair the person keeps stays flagged.
- `GET /api/v1/finance/register/{id}` adds the sources with the **masked SMS text only while the retention setting keeps it** (`body_retention_days`, [Masking and retention](finance-sms.md#masking-and-retention)), the receipts' vault document ids, the rule-based category and type suggestion and what made them (your rule, a built-in rule or none), read on the row's own account kind (a credit on a card is suggested as a card payment; audit C5) and the matching record of a possible duplicate. Attach receipt uploads the file to the document vault (category receipt, private) and links it to the canonical transaction as evidence; Save category sets a manual category, which no rule changes again.
- `GET /api/v1/finance/accounts/overview`: per account the sources that feed it (`manual`, `sms` when its last four digits or the default account are mapped on Finance, iPhone SMS, `apple_wallet` for a mapped Wallet card, `statement` once a statement was imported), the last balance a bank message stated against the ledger's, the last import and reconciliation, what needs attention (`balance_differs`, `links_need_review`, `possible_duplicates`), a card's limit (from the SMS card mapping) and its next bill linked to that card within 60 days. Plus the review queue count, the newest reconciliation and the ledger kind.
- `GET /api/v1/finance/accounts/{id}/activity?days=7|14|30|90`: money in and out per day, in the account's currency.

**The table on narrow windows.** Amount never leaves the screen: the table gives up columns as its card narrows (Account and Receipt under 1366 px, Source under 1280 px), from 1100 px down the category, type and status show under the merchant, and on phones the date does too; the row's drawer keeps every field (`financeRegister.css`, `e2e/finance.spec.ts`). With no account yet the page says "Add an account first" with a button to Accounts, and **Record transaction** and **Download CSV** carry the reason as their description. The Add transaction dialog checks account, date, amount and description itself and shows a translated message under each field.

**The period on Transactions and ledger** starts on the current month **in the Finance time zone** (Finance settings, `timezone`; UTC only when it cannot be read), not the browser's UTC month: a UAE evening on the last day of a month is still that month. The chosen period is kept in the page's URL (`#finance-transactions?month=2026-10`, `month=all` for every month), so a reload, Back or a shared link opens the same month. When the month shown has no rows (and no filter narrows it) but the ledger has rows in another month, the page says "No transactions in <month>; your latest records are in <month>" with a button that opens that month; it never jumps on its own. This closes the case where a statement imported for last month looked "not showing" because the page opened on an empty current month.

The scope switch on both pages shows this workspace's ledger kind (Finance, iPhone SMS, Settings); each workspace keeps its own ledger, so the other options are disabled and say where that money lives. Import statement and the Imports tab open the existing CSV and PDF import; SMS review is the review queue of [iPhone SMS](finance-sms.md#review-queue), whose settings stay on the SMS capture page. Not built from the mockups: per-account "pause" and "hide from safe-to-spend" (no such setting exists), a stored "not a duplicate" decision, and live bank sync (no provider is connected).

## Safe to spend

`GET /api/v1/finance/safe-to-spend` (stage 3, `finance/safe_to_spend.py`): what the person can spend from today to the end of this month (in their own time zone), and per day, per currency, line by line, from their own records only. It is arithmetic, not advice and not a forecast; the page and the assistant say so.

1. **Money you have:** the balance of every bank and cash account that is not archived. Cards, loans and investments are listed under "Not counted" with the reason (a card's debt is covered by its minimum payment; an investment is not spending money); so are archived accounts.
2. **Bills:** every active bill's due dates from today to the month's end without a matched payment (a weekly bill can count several times; one paid early is not taken off again), and this month's passed due dates that still have none (`bill_overdue`: the money has not gone out yet).
3. **Minimum debt payments:** each debt's minimum payment (at most what is owed now) if its due day is today or later this month, or if it has no due day. Once the due day has passed it is paid only when a payment is matched to the debt this month ([Debts and goals](#debts-and-goals), `minimum_passed`, listed as not counted); without one it stays counted and says overdue (`minimum_overdue` with the date). A debt kept on a card account is checked the same way, so match the card payment to the debt. A minimum also saved as a bill is listed as not counted (`minimum_in_bill`): the bill counts it once. Money borrowed from a person counts the amount left when its due date is in this month (`minimum_due`) or has passed (`minimum_overdue`); without a due date it is not counted here.
4. **Goals:** the monthly amount the person set on the goal (`monthly_contribution`), else what is left divided by the months left to the target date, this month included, rounded **up** to the minor unit. A goal without either, or already reached, is not counted.
5. **Buffer:** what the person always keeps back, per currency (`PUT /plan`).

`safe_to_spend = money you have - bills - minimum payments - goals - buffer`. A negative figure is shown as "short this month by", with what to change. `per_day` divides a positive figure by the days left, today included, rounded **down** to the minor unit, so it never allows more than the month does. Each line carries a `note` code and `params` for the clients to word in English or Arabic, and an English `detail` for the assistant.

**One total.** With `combine_currencies` on (`PUT /plan`), the response adds `combined` in the base currency when every currency's figure can be converted with a recorded rate no older than `fx_max_age_days` (default 7): each conversion lists the amount, the rate, its date, its source and the shared base of a cross rate (for example USD to AED through EUR). If a rate is missing the status is `missing_rates` with the pairs, and the page points to Connections. Otherwise every figure stays per currency.

## Forecast

`GET /api/v1/finance/forecast` (`finance/forecast.py`, pure; route and Suggestions producer in `finance/forecast_api.py`): where the person's bank and cash money is expected to be, **day by day**, to the end of this month and over a **three-month horizon** (this month and the two after it), per currency. It replaces the web-only runway that Safe to spend used to draw from two month summaries and the upcoming bills (audit: "Month-end forecast 2, web only, ignores expected income"); the web and the phone read this one answer. It is an **estimate, never a promise**: today's balance is labelled actual, everything after it expected, and every assumption is written out with its basis. Nothing here moves money.

It reuses the shared figures, never a second calculation:

| Input | From | When |
|---|---|---|
| Start | Today's balance of every bank and cash account that is not archived (`commitment_math.liquid`, the money Safe to spend counts); cards, loans and investments are not cash | Today, actual |
| Salary | The [salary profile](#salary): the expected net (gross less the deductions active that month less the advance repayment; a payslip for the month overrides it) on each usual pay day | On the pay day. This month's salary is left out once a credit for it is found (it is in the balance already, `salary_received`); a month the salary check calls missing is listed under "Not counted" (`salary_missing`); one a few days late but not missing yet counts today (`late`) |
| Other recurring income | Money in from one payer, monthly, three times or more, amounts within 15 percent (the bill finder's rule, `summary.detect_recurring`, read on money in), never the salary, a transfer or money lent or borrowed | On the next dates by its cadence; the low line leaves it out |
| Bills | Every active bill's due dates without a matched payment (`obligations.unpaid`) | On the due day; this month's passed ones without a payment count today (`overdue`) |
| Debt minimums | `commitment_math.debt_minimum` each month (at most what is left, so a small debt stops), a minimum also saved as a bill counted once as the bill; money borrowed from a person counts what is left on its due date | On the due day; a day passed this month with no matched payment counts today (`overdue`), one with a matched payment is paid; no due day: today this month, the 1st after |
| Installments | Unpaid installments from the provider statements (Tamara, Tabby), less refunds applied; an order saved as a bill is the bill | On the due date; this month's passed ones count today |
| Regular transfers | Remittances marked "every month": the newest amount plus fee per recipient (one saved as a bill is the bill) | On the same day each month; not again this month once one was sent |
| Expected one-off income | What the person entered under Expected one-off income on Safe to spend (`GET|POST /expected-income`, `PATCH|DELETE /expected-income/{id}`, table `finance_expected_income`): a bonus, a refund or a sale | On its date until it is marked received (`received_on`); a date that passed without that is listed under "Not counted" (`expected_income_late`); the low line leaves it out |
| Card interest | Each card debt with a known yearly rate (`apr_percent`): the balance left after that month's minimum times the rate over 12, added to what is owed so the next month's interest is on it too (`card_interest`, `InterestCharge`; each month's total in `months[].card_interest`) | On the card's due day (the month's last day without one), from today. Owed to the card: shown beside the cash line, never taken from bank money until paid. A card with a balance and no rate is listed under "Not counted" (`card_apr_unknown`), never guessed |
| Usual spending | The average monthly spending from bank and cash accounts over the last three full months (the advisor's averaging rule), less the saved bills in the same categories, so rent is not counted twice | Every day from tomorrow; with too little history none is taken off and the note says the line is too high. Card purchases are left to the card's minimum |

**The band.** The low line spends at the busiest averaged month's pace and leaves out other recurring income; the high line spends at the quietest month's pace. With fewer than two full months the band is 10 percent of the spending either way. Each point carries `expected`, `low` and `high`; each month (`months`, this month first) its expected, low and high balance on the last day, the money in and out, and the spending taken off.

**Warnings**, each with the first date: `below_zero` (the expected line goes below zero), `below_reserve` (below the [emergency reserve](#emergency-reserve) target in that currency) and `may_go_below_zero` (only the low line does). Different currencies are never added or converted: a currency with a bill or a salary and no cash account starts at zero, and says so by going below it.

**In the Suggestions queue.** The `forecast` producer adds a `cash_shortage` suggestion (`params.reason` `below_zero` or `below_reserve`, with the date, the balance and the threshold; key `cash_shortage:forecast:<currency>:<reason>:<month>`) next to Safe to spend's own shortage. It reads the forecast in its own short units of work and writes nothing; Acknowledge only records that the owner saw it.

**Clients.** Web Safe to spend (`FinanceForecast.tsx` `ForecastCard` and `ExpectedIncomeCard`, helpers `lib/financeForecast.ts`): the expected one-off income (add, edit, mark received, delete; English and Arabic), today's actual, the expected balance on the month end with its range, the money in and out, the warning (with Change the reserve for a reserve warning), the chart with the band and the reserve line (This month or 3 months), what was assumed and left out, and every expected movement; the Finance overview's Safe to spend card shows the expected month end and a warning. The phone's Safe to spend shows the expected month end, its range and the warning, and **Expected one-off income** (list with Show received ones too and paging, add, edit, Mark received with today's date, delete after a confirm; each change reloads the forecast; `apps/mobile/lib/features/finance/finance_lending_view.dart` `ExpectedIncomeView`). Tests: `services/api/tests/test_finance_forecast.py` (salary on pay day with deductions and an advance, a salary already received, a missing salary, an overdue minimum, a minimum paid this month, a card minimum that stops, a bill, an installment, regular remittances, recurring income with confidence, the band, never mixing currencies, the below-reserve warning with its date, the route, the Suggestions item, other people and viewers), `tests/test_finance_lending_depth.py` (loan schedules and interest by hand, overdue and repayment suggestions, card interest, expected income), `tests/test_postgres_finance_depth.py`, `tests/test_postgres_finance_forecast.py` (the reserve months stored under forced RLS, private to the owner, refused by the database on another purpose), web `test/financeForecast.test.ts`, `e2e/finance-forecast.spec.ts`, `e2e/finance-pages.spec.ts`, Flutter `apps/mobile/test/finance_test.dart` (the forecast month end, its range and warning, the reserve progress, a forecast that cannot be read) and `apps/mobile/test/finance_lending_test.dart` (expected income: empty with Add, add, mark received, show received, edit, delete, paging, a refused amount, a failure with Retry, permission denied, and the layouts at 390x844 and 768x1024 in English and Arabic).

## Emergency reserve

The emergency reserve is a **goal with the purpose "emergency"** (`finance_goals.purpose = 'emergency'`), not a table of its own: a goal already has the target, the currency, the account that holds it (`account_id`: a bank, cash or investment account in its currency, whose balance is then what is saved) or the amount saved by hand, the monthly amount, pausing, and it is already exported, deleted, owner-only under forced RLS and counted by Safe to spend and the planner. The only addition is `finance_goals.reserve_months` (migration `0156_finance_reserve`, expand only: a nullable 1 to 24 with a check that only an emergency goal carries it): a reserve **sized in months of essential spending** keeps the months chosen, and its `target_amount` is that many months of today's essential spending, set when it is saved. A reserve sized as a fixed amount has no months.

`GET /api/v1/finance/reserve` (`finance:read`, `forecast.reserves`): per emergency goal the target (what Safe to spend and the forecast use), the mode and months, the **recalculated target** (months x today's essential spending, so the page can say the essentials moved and offer to update), the essential monthly figure and its basis (the advisor's runway: bills, debt minimums and the essential categories; the bare minimum of bills and minimums with less than 30 days of records), what is saved now (the account's balance when it is held in one), the progress, **how many months of essentials it covers** (saved / essential, one decimal, rounded down), where it is held, paused and the status (`reached`, `building`, `empty`). Currencies with essential spending and no reserve get a suggested target of three months (`planner.RESERVE_MONTHS`). The reserve is saved through the goals routes (`POST`, `PATCH /goals` with `purpose: "emergency"` and `reserve_months`, 0 for a fixed amount; left out on a PATCH it is kept); a reserve in months on another purpose is refused. Its target is the [forecast's](#forecast) reserve line and its `below_reserve` warning.

Clients: web Safe to spend and Finance settings (`ReserveCard`: saved of target, a progress meter, months covered, the mode, where it is held, paused), Finance settings' reserve form (currency, months of essentials or a fixed amount with the target worked out from `GET /reserve`, kept in an account or in the goal, saved so far, the monthly amount); the phone's Safe to spend shows the progress and months covered with Change the reserve, and the phone's Finance settings carry the same form per currency (months of essentials or a fixed amount, kept in an account or in the goal, saved so far, the monthly amount; `apps/mobile/lib/features/finance/finance_depth_view.dart`).

## Money advisor

A weekly money note for each person: where the money went, how many days the money would last if income stopped, the payments coming in the next 30 days, advice on each card and a short ranked list of next steps. **ANUM only advises: it never moves money, pays a bill or touches a bank or card.** The page says so on every view, the notice and the push say so, and no route in it writes anything but the note and its switches.

Code: `finance/advisor.py` (pure arithmetic, no I/O, no model call), `finance/advisor_text.py` (the English and Arabic step templates), `finance/advisor_jobs.py` (gathering, writing and the weekly job), `finance/advisor_store.py` and `db/advisor_repository.py`, `finance/advisor_api.py`; migration `0143_finance_money_advisor`; web `apps/web/src/FinanceAdvisorPage.tsx` (`#finance-advisor`), `lib/financeAdvisor.ts`. Tests: `services/api/tests/test_finance_advisor.py`, `test_postgres_finance_advisor.py`, `apps/web/test/financeAdvisor.test.ts`, `apps/web/e2e/finance-advisor.spec.ts`.

Everything is per currency: nothing is ever added or converted across currencies. Amounts are exact decimals. The inputs are the person's own records only: accounts and their ledger balances, the transactions of the last 400 days (statement imports, PDF imports and iPhone SMS all land there), bills, debts, the credit limits entered in iPhone SMS settings and the last balance a bank message stated.

### Survival runway

If income stopped, how long would the money last? **Money you have** is the balance of every bank and cash account that is not archived. A card's available limit is not money you have: it is shown on its own and never counted. Three ways:

| Scenario | Monthly outflow |
|---|---|
| Bare minimum | Active recurring bills as a monthly figure (weekly x 52 / 12, quarterly / 3, yearly / 12; a one-off bill is not monthly) plus every debt's minimum payment (at most what is owed) |
| Essentials | Bare minimum plus the average monthly spending in housing, utilities, telecom, groceries, transport, fuel, health and education. A saved bill in one of those categories is taken out of that category's average, so it is never counted twice |
| As you spend now | The average of every spending category plus minimums, never less than essentials |

The average is over the last three full calendar months the records cover (a month counts when the records start in its first week); with no full month, the last 30 days scaled to a month. Days are `money you have x 30.4375 / monthly outflow`, rounded down, with the date the money runs out. No money gives 0 days; nothing going out gives no end date; a runway past the last date the calendar holds keeps its day count and shows no end date. **With fewer than 30 days of records** only the bare minimum is worked out (it needs no history) and the note says plainly that there is not enough history, instead of guessing. The page writes out every assumption under the figures. Salary detection only informs: a credit with salary, payroll, WPS or راتب in the last 60 days, or a steady monthly credit from one payer; without one the note says so, and the runway already assumes no income.

### Where money went

Spending is money out except transfers and income. The **last 7 days** (the week ending yesterday) against the 7 days before and against the three-month average for a week; **this month so far** against the same days of last month and the three-month monthly average. Top categories (7 days, this month, usual month), the top five payees for the week and the month, and **unusual** spending: a category already 30 % above a usual whole month (so a rent paid on the 1st is not flagged), or a category with nothing in the averaged months that took at least 10 % of a usual month in 7 days. Small amounts (under 5 % of a usual month) are not flagged.

### Coming payments

The next 30 days: saved bills (a weekly bill each time it falls due), debt and card minimums on their next due day (a minimum with no due day is listed as "no due day set"), and recurring payments found in the records but not saved as bills (same payee, steady interval and amount, three times or more; the [bill suggestions](#summary) rule), each date in the window by its cadence like a saved bill, marked as subscriptions when the category or payee says so. Totals per currency: saved, found, and due within 7 days.

### Card advice

Each card account (and each card under Debts with no card account). What it owes by the ledger (or the debt's balance when the ledger shows nothing), the last balance a bank message stated, the credit limit and what is available when the person entered a limit, the minimum and the next due date from the linked debt. With an interest rate the person entered (APR on the debt), the interest of paying only the minimum next month (`(owed - minimum) x APR / 12`, rounded up), the months to clear at the minimum and the interest paid until then (each month the minimum is paid first, then interest is charged on what is left, the same order as the next-month figure), or that the minimum never clears the balance. **A rate is never invented:** an APR of 0 or none means "add the rate", and every missing piece is listed (a debt record, the rate, the due day, the minimum, the limit) with where to add it.

### Next steps

At most 10, most urgent first, then the soonest date. Fixed templates in English and Arabic (`advisor_text.py`, figures and names wrapped in Unicode isolates in Arabic, no long dashes), each with the Finance page that does it: pay a card in full before its due date (with the interest the minimum would cost when the rate is known), a minimum that never clears, add a card's debt record, due day, minimum or rate, a card over 30 % of its limit, a bill or minimum due within 7 days, survival under 60 days (urgent under 30), import more history, import a recent statement (newest record over 7 days old), add an account, spending up or new in a category, review subscriptions, save recurring payments as bills, and no salary found. No model writes them.

Both tables have forced RLS with the tenant policy, a restrictive owner-only policy and the tenant purge policy; they are in the organisation lifecycle, in "Download my data" (`advisor_notes`, `advisor_settings` in the `finance` section) and in "Delete my data". Notes older than two years are deleted when a new one is written.

## Bills and budgets

Finance, **Bills and budgets** (`#finance-bills`, `#finance-budgets`; web `FinanceBillsBudgets.tsx`, figures in `lib/financeBills.ts`) has the tabs Bills, Budgets, Recurring and Calendar, the scope (Personal only: Business and All finance point to the business books, where business bills and budgets live), the month and Add bill.

- **A bill is a forecast until it is matched.** Each due date of an active bill in the window (`GET /bill-schedule?from&to`, at most 400 days; dates run forward from `next_due_on` and back from it, never before the bill was added) has a status worked out on every read: `paid` only when a recorded payment is matched to that due date, `review` when the match waits for review (the payment was corrected), else `overdue`, `due_soon` (7 days or less) or `planned`. Nothing else marks a bill paid.
- **Matching** is a [finance link](finance-links.md) in the `bill` dimension: target `finance_bill_due`, id `<bill id>:<YYYY-MM-DD>`, on the payment's `canonical_id`. `GET /payment-candidates?for=bill|debt&around&amount&currency&q` lists the person's own payments from 60 days before to 15 days after the date (money in is never offered), closest in amount first, with what is left to match. `POST /bills/{id}/matches` (`{canonical_id, due_on, amount?}`, optional `Idempotency-Key`) takes the expected amount, at most what is left of the payment; `DELETE /bills/{id}/matches/{link_id}` voids the link. The journal is never written.
- **Budgets** are the person's Guardian category caps. `GET /budgets?month` gives each cap with what was spent, what is left, `ok`, `near` or `over`, and for the current month where this month's pace ends (`projected`, `projected_over`), plus the day by day spend of the capped categories against the caps together (the chart). A new or changed cap is proposed and confirmed with its code like every rule ([Guardian](#guardian)); removing one waits in Guardian. `PUT /budgets/alerts` keeps, per cap, the alert threshold (50 to 99 percent, default 80) and whether to alert at it and at 100 percent; Guardian's `cap_near` and `cap_over` alerts follow them.
- The page shows the KPIs (bills this month, due in 7 days or overdue, active budgets, budget remaining, per currency, never added across currencies), the day strip and the bills table, Budget health with the spend chart (a table behind "Show as a table"), the budget most at risk, Budget rules, the Link and pay explainer, the recurring bills (pause, resume, edit, delete, the detected recurring payments to keep) and the month as a calendar.

## Debts and goals

Finance, **Debts and goals** (`#finance-debts`, `#finance-goals`; web `FinanceDebtsGoals.tsx`, the payoff from the API's one engine, display helpers in `lib/financeDebtPlan.ts`) has the tabs Debts, Goals, Plan and History, the scope (Personal only), the currency (one plan per currency, never mixed) and the New plan wizard.

- **The plan** (`GET, PUT /debt-plan`, `{plans: [{currency, strategy, monthly_payment}]}`): avalanche (highest rate first) or snowball (smallest balance first), and the monthly payment for all debts of that currency (empty: the minimums only). Stored with the safe-to-spend settings (`finance_plans.debt_plans`) without changing them.
- **The maths** is the API's one payoff engine (`finance/planner.py` behind `GET /planner/payoff?currency&payment&minimums_only`), the same answer the Money advisor's Plan view, the assistant and the mobile app read; the web keeps no engine of its own. The page asks it for the saved plan, for the minimums only (`minimums_only=true`: each debt gets only its own minimum, nothing rolls over), for a previewed payment, for the what-if (the plan's payment plus the extra) and in the wizard. Each strategy's answer carries the payoff month of every debt (`paid_off_in`) and the total owed at the end of each of the next 12 months (`balances`) for the outlook. It runs on the debts with today's balances: each month a twelfth of the yearly rate on the balance is added (rounded to the minor unit), every debt gets its minimum, the rest of the payment goes in strategy order, and a paid-off debt frees its minimum for the next. It gives the payoff month with the minimums only and with the plan, the interest, the payoff order, the what-if (an extra amount a month: months sooner and interest saved) and the 12-month outlook of total debt and goal savings. A payment below the minimums together is refused; payments that never cover the interest say so instead of a date. The page says plainly that rates and balances are the person's own estimates.
- **Payoff progress** needs a starting balance: `finance_debts.original_balance` (filled with today's balance when a debt is added on the web; left out of an edit, it is kept; `""` clears it).
- **Debt payments** are links in the `debt` dimension (target `finance_debt`): `POST /debts/{id}/payments`, `DELETE /debts/{id}/payments/{link_id}`, `GET /debt-payments?debt_id&limit&offset` (date, account, amount, status and what was paid per debt). The stored balance stays what the person entered; every figure that reads it (the summary, net worth, Safe to spend, the advisor and the ledger dashboard) takes off the principal of the payments matched since it was saved, with the interest estimated from the person's own rate ([How figures are counted](#how-figures-are-counted)). A debt kept on a card or loan account follows that account instead.
- **Goals** have a purpose (`finance_goals.purpose`: general, emergency fund, family at home, a purchase, education, travel; migration `0147_finance_personal_money`); the planner reads the emergency fund when it says what to avoid. Goals show progress, the monthly target (the amount set, else what is left over the months left) and the due date. `finance_goals.paused`: a paused goal keeps what is saved, is not set aside by safe to spend (`goal_paused`, under "Not counted") and is left out of the commitments and the outlook. Upcoming commitments list each debt's next minimum payment and the goal contributions for the month.

Code: `finance/commitments.py` (pure figures and the link targets), `finance/commitments_api.py` (routes); migration `0142_finance_debts_bills` (expand only: `paused`, `original_balance`, the two plan columns and the `bill` and `debt` link dimensions); contracts `packages/contracts/src/financePlanning.ts`. Tests: `tests/test_finance_debts_bills.py`, `tests/test_finance_payoff.py` (the engine), `tests/test_postgres_finance_debts_bills.py`, web `test/financeDebtPlan.test.ts`, `test/financeBills.test.ts`, `e2e/finance-planning.spec.ts`.

Not built yet: bill reminders a set number of days before the due date (a daily job like the purchase reminders: the mockup's Reminder column is left out until it exists), splitting a debt payment into interest and principal from the lender's own figures (the split is estimated from the rate the person entered), and business bills and budgets on these pages (they are in the business books).

## Bank loans

Finance, Debts and goals, **Loans** (`#finance-debts-loans`; web `FinanceBankLoan.tsx`, helpers `lib/financeLoans.ts`; a debt's menu opens "Loan schedule and history" or "Add loan terms"): a bank or finance loan (for example an Al Rajhi car or personal loan in SAR) entered with its dates, its whole schedule, the payments already taken for it and what is left. It extends the existing Debts: the terms are nullable columns on `finance_debts` (migration `0161_finance_bank_loans`), nothing else stores a loan.

- **Terms** (`PUT /debts/{id}/loan`, `DELETE /debts/{id}/loan`; `finance:manage`): the lender, the start date (the day it was paid out) and the amount borrowed, the rate type (`reducing` or `flat`) and the yearly rate, the term in months, the lender's instalment when known, the day of the month it is paid, how it is repaid (`bank_debit` from a bank or cash account in the loan's currency, which is then required; `salary_deduction`; `manual`), and the status: `open`, `paid_off` or `closed` with its date. A schedule needs the start date, the amount and the term or the instalment. A rate is more than 0 and at most 100, or empty when it is not known. Removing the terms makes the typed balance count again; linked payments stay.
- **The schedule** (`finance/bank_loans.py`, pure): every instalment from the month after the start to the end, with its date, principal, interest and the balance after. Reducing: the planner's annuity (`planner.amortised`), a twelfth of the yearly rate on what is left each month (half to even to the minor unit), the last instalment takes what is left. Flat: the profit `amount x rate x years` of `planner.compare`, spread evenly, each instalment repaying the same share of the amount. The lender's instalment wins over the computed one. **A rate is never invented**: without one the instalments are the lender's (or the amount over the term) and their split into principal and interest is unknown (`basis: plain`); what is left is then the instalments not paid yet. An instalment that never covers the interest is refused (`never_ends`).
- **Paid** comes only from what the owner accepted or picked: payments linked to the debt (`debt` dimension, a bank debit or any payment matched by hand) and the salary months of an accepted batch. Each payment is placed on the instalment nearest its date (within 20 days), else on the first one not paid yet; more than an instalment covers the next. Each instalment is `paid` (with its payment), `part_paid`, `missed` (5 days after its date without a payment), `upcoming`, `ambiguous` (more than one debit fits; pick one) or `settled` (after the day it was paid off or closed).
- **Totals**: paid so far, interest paid (unknown without a rate), the balance left (the amount less the principal paid; the instalments not paid without a rate; nothing once paid off or closed), the payoff date (the last instalment, or the day it was paid off or closed) and months ahead or behind (instalments paid in full less those due by today). `GET /loans` lists every loan with terms, open first; `GET /debts/{id}/loan` gives the terms, every instalment, the totals and the accepted batches (`finance:read`, the owner's own: another person's debt is not found).
- **Finding past deductions** (`finance/bank_loan_service.py`, the Suggestions producer `bank_loans`, kind `loan_payments`). For `bank_debit`: money out of the chosen account, from the start date to today, of the instalment's amount within 1 percent, posted within 5 days of the instalment's date, not linked to a debt yet, whose description does not name another merchant (`merchant_match`; naming the lender decides between two). One debit pays one instalment (the nearest). For `salary_deduction`: that month's payslip's `loan` deductions, or, without a payslip for the month, the salary profile's `loan` deduction rule once the pay day passed; a payslip without the deduction is a missing month. One suggestion per loan, "N payments found for <loan>", lists every instalment due so far as found, missing or ambiguous. **Accept** (`POST /suggestions/accept` with the payload hash shown) links every found bank debit through the links API (every link check applies; a refused one voids those made and releases the decision) and records the salary months, as one batch (`finance_loan_batches`); `POST /loan-batches/{id}/undo` voids every link it made, forgets its salary months and brings the suggestion back. Ambiguous months wait for a pick on the loan page (`POST /debts/{id}/payments`). As new deductions arrive, a new suggestion lists them. Nothing is linked without that accept, and no automation policy may accept it (`link_loan_payments` is in the never-automated list).
- **Counted once**: see [How figures are counted](#how-figures-are-counted). A loan deducted from the salary is not counted again when the salary profile or this month's payslip already takes a loan deduction.
- **History**: a loan paid off or closed leaves the Debts list and the plan and stays under Loans and History ("Loans paid off or closed") with its whole record.

Tests: `tests/test_finance_bank_loans.py` (the maths, a 24-month loan with one missing and one ambiguous month, salary deductions, accept and undo, no double counting, refusals), `tests/test_postgres_finance_bank_loans.py`, web `test/financeLoans.test.ts`, `e2e/finance-bank-loans.spec.ts`. Migration `0161_finance_bank_loans` (expand only, after `0160_finance_depth_2`): twelve nullable columns on `finance_debts` with database checks, and `finance_loan_batches` with forced RLS (`tenant_isolation_`, restrictive `owner_only_`, `lifecycle_purge_`), listed in `tenant_lifecycle_tables.py` and exported with Finance (`loan_batches`). The previous release ignores both and reads the typed balance. Rollback: `alembic downgrade 0160_finance_depth_2` (test databases only; production restores a backup).

Not built: reading the lender's own statement or amortisation table (the schedule is worked out from the terms); early-settlement rebates on flat loans (a paid-off loan is simply marked paid off with its date). **On the phone** (Finance, Debts, Bank loans, or More in Finance; `finance_loans_screens.dart`): every loan with what is left, the next instalment and whether it is ahead or behind; a loan's summary (instalment, total to repay and interest, paid, interest paid, left, payoff date, instalments paid), its terms, the schedule (the first 12, then Show all) with each instalment's principal, interest, paid amount and state, **Pick the payment** for an instalment more than one debit fits (`POST /debts/{id}/payments` with an `Idempotency-Key`), the accepted batches with **Undo** after a confirmation (`POST /loan-batches/{id}/undo`), **Add loan terms** to a debt that has none, **Edit terms** (the full `PUT /debts/{id}/loan` body, the bank account picked from the person's bank and cash accounts in the loan's currency) and **Remove terms** after a confirmation. Tests: `apps/mobile/test/finance/finance_more_test.dart`.

## Lent and borrowed

Finance, **Lent and borrowed** (the People section of [Debts and goals](#debts-and-goals), `#finance-debts-people`; the old `#finance-people` opens it there; web `FinancePersonalMoney.tsx` `PeopleSection`): loans with people, interest free (qard hasan) unless the person sets a rate, in both directions: **I lent** (they owe me) and **I borrowed** (I owe them). Each loan (`finance_person_loans`) has the person, amount and currency, the date, an optional due date, notes, the reminder switch with how many days before the due date (0 to 30, default 3) and "closed without full repayment" (forgiven or settled another way).

- **Schedule and interest** (migration `0158_finance_depth`, `personal.loan_schedule`). A loan is repaid once on its due date, or on a monthly schedule: `installments` (1 to 360) from `first_due_on`, each on the same day of the month (the month's last day when shorter); the due date is then the last repayment. An optional `interest_rate` (a yearly percentage, more than 0 and at most 100; empty is interest free): on a schedule, equal payments (an annuity at the rate over 12 on what is left each month); on a single due date, simple interest for the days from the date the money changed hands (a 365-day year). Every repayment is rounded to the currency's minor unit and the last takes the rounding. Interest without a due date or a schedule is refused (nothing to work it out on). The detail lists the schedule (`schedule`: number, date, principal, interest, amount and `repaid`, `part_repaid`, `overdue` or `due`).
- **What is owed and what is overdue.** Repaid money (the active repayment links in the loan's currency only: money in another currency is never linked, so it never reduces what is owed) covers the schedule in date order. `outstanding` is the amount plus the scheduled interest less what is repaid; `next_due_on` and `next_due_amount` the first repayment not covered yet; `overdue_amount` what was due before today and is not repaid; the status is `overdue` while that is more than zero. The list's `owed` gives, per currency and never added across currencies, what they owe you and what you owe with the overdue part of each (`they_owe_me_overdue`, `i_owe_overdue`).
- **Suggestions** (`finance/loan_suggestions.py`, producer `person_loans`): `loan_overdue` names an overdue repayment (Acknowledge only); `loan_repayment` proposes a transaction from any source (statement, CSV, PDF, SMS, Apple Pay or typed) as a repayment when it is money in for money lent or money out for money borrowed, in the loan's currency, on or after the day the money changed hands, within the last 120 days, not linked to a loan yet, at most what is left, and either names the person (merchant words agree; a row read as a refund only then) or is exactly the next repayment. One loan only, or nothing is proposed. Accept makes the link through the links API with every link check ([Suggestions](#suggestions)); nothing is linked before that, and ANUM never asks anyone for money or pays anyone.
- **One payment split across loans** (`POST /people-loans/split`, `{canonical_id, kind: repayment|handed, parts: [{loan_id, amount}]}`, 2 to 10 parts, optional `Idempotency-Key`; `links.create_many`). One transaction can repay several loans with an amount each: every part is in the transaction's own currency (loans in different currencies are refused, `currency_mismatch`), all the loans go the same way (`mixed_direction`), each loan appears once (`duplicate_part`), no part is more than that loan still expects (`409 over_loan`), and the parts together are no more than what is left of the transaction in the `person_loan` dimension (`409 over_allocated` with `remaining`). Every check runs before any part is written, in one unit of work under the owner's lock, so all parts are made or none; the database's amount check covers the total at commit. A retry with the same key returns the parts already made (`replayed`). Unlinking removes one part (`DELETE /people-loans/{id}/links/{link_id}`); the other parts stay. The `loan_repayment` suggestion proposes a split when one transaction is exactly the repayments due now (what is overdue, else the next repayment) on two or more open loans with the same person, earliest due first, and the words name the person (0.85) or name no one (0.6); words that name someone else, or a refund that names no one, give none. Its action carries `parts` (one link proposal per loan, sealed in the payload hash) and Accept makes them through `links.create_many` (`link_ids` in the answer). Web: the loan's detail has **Split one payment across loans** (pick the payment, then an amount per open loan with the person in that currency, each starting at what is due on it; the dialog says what the API would refuse before sending); the Suggestions card lists the parts.
- **Linked to the ledger.** What changed hands and every repayment are [finance links](finance-links.md) in the `person_loan` dimension (target `finance_person_loan`) on real recorded transactions: lending and repaying a loan are money out (`payment`), borrowing and being repaid are money in (`deposit`); a transaction in the wrong direction is refused (`not_a_payment`, `not_money_in`). Repaid is the sum of the active repayment links in the loan's currency; left is the amount less that. Status: `open`, `due_soon` (7 days or less), `overdue`, `repaid` or `closed`.
- **Not spending, not income.** A linked transaction is a transfer everywhere ([How figures are counted](#how-figures-are-counted)). Deleting a loan voids its links; the transactions stay in the ledger and count by their category again.
- **Borrowed money is a debt, lent money a receivable.** An open loan borrowed from a person joins the Debts plan's payoff order (`GET /planner/payoff`, `source: person_loan`, at the loan's rate, 0 when interest free), Safe to spend and Monthly fixed costs when it is due (`source: person_loan`, note `due_this_month`), the advisor's coming payments and net worth; money lent counts in net worth as owed back. A person also saved as a Debt is counted once, as the Debt (`obligations.borrowed_debts`, tests `tests/test_finance_person_debts.py`).
- **Reminders** in ANUM only: one notification `remind_days_before` days before the next repayment's date (the due date without a schedule) and one more once it is overdue, for each repayment date of a schedule (kind `finance_loan_due`, in-app only: never an email and never a phone push). The daily job (`python -m anum_api.voice_retention`, reported under `finance_loan_reminders`, counts only; `finance/personal_jobs.py`) finds people the way the weekly money note does (an active finance account) **and** by an open loan with a due date and reminders on, so someone who only records loans, with no finance account, is reminded too (`discover_loan_people`; migration `0179_loan_reminder_discovery` lets `anum_maintenance` read only the scope columns and `remind`, `due_on` and `closed_on` of `finance_person_loans`, never the person, amount or notes, and never write). It works in each person's own scope; the due date announced is claimed with a compare and set before the notice and released if the notice fails, so each reminder goes out once. Changing the due date re-arms it. A viewer gets none. The reminder goes to the person who recorded the loan, through ANUM's own notifications, and the loan's reminder switch is the opt-out. It is never sent to the other person (the friend who owes or is owed): ANUM holds no contact or consent for them, and a message to them would be an outward send needing an approval of its own, which is not built.
- Routes: `GET /people-loans?q&direction&status&limit&offset` (with what is owed per currency over every open loan and the overdue count), `POST /people-loans`, `GET|PATCH|DELETE /people-loans/{id}` (the detail lists the linked transactions and the schedule; the body takes `interest_rate`, `installments` and `first_due_on`), `POST /people-loans/{id}/links` (`{canonical_id, kind: handed|repayment, amount?}`, optional `Idempotency-Key`: a retry of a link that succeeded returns that link, `replayed: true`, also for payslips and remittances), `DELETE /people-loans/{id}/links/{link_id}`. `GET /payment-candidates?for=person_loan&direction=in|out` offers the person's own transactions.

## Salary

The **salary profile** and the month by month salary check (`finance/salary.py`, routes `finance/salary_api.py`; stored next to the payslips in `finance_salary_profiles`, migration `0150_finance_salary_profile`, forced RLS with the tenant and owner-only policies, exported and deleted with Finance). One profile per person.

- **The profile:** the employer, the joining date, the expected monthly salary (gross) and its currency, the usual pay day (the month's last day when shorter) and the account it is paid into (none: any bank or cash account in its currency). `GET /salary/profile` (`{profile: null}` until added; with this month's `expected_net`, `next_pay_on` and what is left of each advance), `PUT /salary/profile` (a joining date in the future, an account in another currency, or an advance repaid faster than it was given is refused), `DELETE /salary/profile` (payslips and transactions stay).
- **Rules** (`rules` on the profile): a **deduction** taken off every month from its first month to its last (reason `loan`, `absence`, `social_insurance` such as GOSI, or `other`), and a **salary advance**: received now (`received_on`, `total`) and repaid by `amount` a month from its first month until the total is back. Expected net for a month is gross less the deductions active that month less the advance repayment. **A payslip for the month overrides it** ([Payslips](#payslips)): its gross and deductions are that month's figures.
- **Detection across every source:** for each month from the joining month (at most 36), money in on the salary account in its currency, whether it came from a statement, an SMS or was typed, from 7 days before to 10 days after the pay day. Each candidate is scored on the amount (against the latest confirmed salary, else the expected net), the day and the payer (the same payer as a confirmed salary, else the employer's name or a salary word such as salary, payroll, WPS or راتب); the best is suggested with its confidence (high, medium or low). Money lent or borrowed with a person and the credit of an advance are never a salary. A credit linked to that month's payslip is the salary.
- **Confirm or reject** (`POST /salary/months/{month}/decision {transaction_id, decision: confirm|reject}`, undo with `DELETE /salary/months/{month}/decision?transaction_id`): a rejected transaction is remembered and never suggested for that month again; a confirmed one is the salary and its payer is how later months are recognised.
- **Expected against received** (`GET /salary/months?limit&offset`, newest first): each month's expected gross, deductions, advance repayment and net, what was received and the difference, the flags `late` (more than 3 days after the pay day), `partial` (under 95 percent of the expected net) and `missing` ("No salary found for October 2026, expected around 27 October"), `upcoming` before the pay day, and how many months wait for a decision.
- **One income figure** (`salary.monthly_income`): with a salary profile in that currency, this month's expected net; otherwise the average monthly income of the last three full months with records ([How figures are counted](#how-figures-are-counted)). The Money advisor (`income.monthly`, `source`, `next_pay_on`, `missing_months` and the action "No salary found for ..."), the planner's affordability ratio ([Planning and advice](#planning-and-advice)), Safe to spend's payday (`next_pay_on`, `expected_pay`, `days_to_payday` on the salary's currency) and the share Monthly fixed costs take of income (`income` on `GET /fixed-commitments`) all read it.

**On the phone** (Finance, More in Finance, Salary profile, or Edit the salary profile in Finance settings; `finance_salary_screen.dart`): the profile with this month's expected net and the next pay day, **Edit the profile** (employer, joining date, expected gross, currency, pay day, the account it is paid into; `rules: null` keeps the deductions and advances), **Add a deduction** (reason, label, amount a month, first and last month) and **Add a salary advance** (the amount received, when, the monthly repayment and its first month) with Edit and Remove on each, and **Delete the profile** after a confirmation; each change sends the whole profile and the salary cards reload. Tests: `apps/mobile/test/finance/finance_money_test.dart`.

Tests: `tests/test_finance_salary.py`, `tests/test_postgres_finance_personal_money.py`.

## Fixed commitments

Finance, **Monthly fixed costs** (a section of [Bills and budgets](#bills-and-budgets), `#finance-bills-fixed`; the old `#finance-commitments` opens it there; `GET /fixed-commitments`; `personal.fixed_commitments`): everything that goes out every month, one total per currency, never added across currencies:

- active saved bills by their monthly figure (weekly x 52 / 12, quarterly / 3, yearly / 12), shown as rent (housing, room rent), subscriptions or bills; a one-off bill is listed as not monthly;
- installment plans from the imported provider statements (Tamara, Tabby): this month's unpaid installments per order;
- debt minimums (loans and cards, at most what is owed), and money borrowed from a person in the month it is due (the amount left; `source: person_loan`, note `due_this_month`);

## Planning and advice

The **Plan** view inside the Money advisor (`#finance-advisor`, tab Plan; web `FinanceAdvisorPlan.tsx`) and the assistant's planning tools answer "can I afford this", "which debt first", "which loan" and "the cheapest way to send money home" from the person's own records. **Advice only: ANUM never pays, buys or transfers.** Every figure is labelled as the person's own data or as a live source with its address and the date it was read. Code: `finance/planner.py` (pure arithmetic), `finance/planning_service.py` (reading the records, live research), `finance/personal_api.py` (routes), `finance/plan_tools.py` (the assistant).

- **Which debt to pay first** (`GET /planner/payoff?payment&currency`): avalanche (highest rate first) against snowball (smallest balance first) with today's balances ([How figures are counted](#how-figures-are-counted)), the rates and the minimums: months to debt-free, total interest and the payoff order for each, which one is cheaper and by how much, and when snowball clears its first debt. The payment is the one asked, else the saved [debt plan](#debts-and-goals), else the minimums (`minimums_only=true` pays only the minimums). The Debts page reads this same engine. The month by month rule: a twelfth of the yearly rate on each balance, rounded half to even to the minor unit; every debt its minimum; the rest in strategy order. A debt with no rate is named and counted at 0 percent, never guessed.
- **Can I afford it** (`POST /planner/afford {price, currency, item, monthly_cost}`): against this month's Safe to spend, money in bank and cash, the monthly income ([the one income figure](#salary): the salary profile's expected net, else the average of the last three full months), the fixed commitments and the essential monthly spending from the advisor's runway. Verdicts: within Safe to spend, from savings, cash leaves under three months of essentials, and for a monthly payment ANUM's rule of thumb (stated as such): commitments up to a third of income are comfortable, up to half tight, over half too much; no income recorded says so. **How to pay** lists the person's bank and cash accounts in that currency that cover it, then cards (interest at the person's own rate, or "add the rate"), then accounts in another currency (FX). **What to avoid** comes only from the records: FX fees the person paid (journal `fx_fee`, 400 days), late fees (a `fees` row that says late, overdue or penalty), carrying a card balance at its rate, commitments over half of income, an emergency fund below three months of essentials. After a purchase the page offers Purchases to record it ([Purchases](purchases.md)).
- **Cash, a loan or installments** (`POST /planner/compare`): for each option the monthly payment, months, total cost (down payment, every payment and fees) and the interest or profit, cheapest first. Standard amortisation `P x r / (1 - (1 + r)^-n)` with `r` the yearly rate over 12, the payment rounded up to the minor unit, or a flat rate (profit `P x rate x years`, common for UAE car and personal finance). An estimate: the lender's offer decides. **A rate is never invented:** the person's own, or a live source with its address (`rate_source`, `https://` only) and date; an option without one is `rate_unknown` and not costed, installments included.
- **Live figures** (`POST /planner/research {topic: price|loan_rate|installments|remittance, query, country}`): one call of the registered [`web_search`](tools-and-integrations.md#web-search) tool (the private SearXNG, `ANUM_SEARXNG_URL`) through the runtime's policy and audit (`business.pipeline.call_tool`, surface `finance_plan`). Each result keeps its address, the date read and the percentages and amounts found in its title and snippet; results are untrusted data. Without web search the answer is `unavailable` ("Live prices are unavailable") and the plan continues with the person's own data; nothing found is `not_found`, the figure unknown. Reading a page in full stays with [browser research](browser-research.md) (`web.browse_page`) when the person gives an address.
- **The cheapest way to send money home**: the person's own provider comparison ([Sending money home](#sending-money-home)) and, when web search is on, today's fees and rates with their sources.

## Foreign-currency balances

For each open bank, cash or investment account in a currency other than the person's base currency, `GET /fx-revaluation?account_id=` (`finance/revaluation.py`, `revaluation_api.py`; web `FinanceFxBalances.tsx` on the Finance overview, Personal and All finance, and on the chosen account of the Accounts page) shows:

- **Worth now**: the balance times the newest stored rate to the base currency ([Exchange rates](#exchange-rates): direct, inverse or a cross, within the person's rate age limit, 7 days unless changed on Safe to spend), with the rate, its date and its source.
- **Worth when it came in**: average cost. The opening balance (on the account's first day) and every credit are valued at the stored rate of their own date; money out takes its share of the cost at the running average, so it needs no rate. Money that only brings an overdrawn balance back to zero carries no cost.
- **Unrealised gain or loss**: worth now less worth when it came in, in the base currency; the page adds the figures it could work out and says how many accounts it left out.

**Policy.** A rate is never guessed: without a rate now the row says "rate not available" (`rate_not_available`) and names how to add one; without a rate on a day money came in it says so with the dates (`cost_rate_not_available`, up to five). The figures are worked out on every read from the stored rates and the ledger, never stored, **never posted** to the ledger or the books and **never counted as spending or income**; they change nothing anywhere else. Cards and loans (money owed) are not revalued, and an account at or below zero has nothing to revalue. The realised difference of an own transfer between currencies stays in the transaction drawer ([Card FX and fees](#card-fx-and-fees)), also shown only.

**On the phone** (Finance, More in Finance, Foreign-currency balances; `finance_tags_screens.dart`): each account with its balance, worth now, worth when it came in, the unrealised gain or loss and the rate with its date and source, the total and how many accounts it leaves out, and the reason a figure is missing in the same words; display only. Tests: `apps/mobile/test/finance/finance_more_test.dart`.

## Exchange rates

`finance/fx.py`. ANUM never invents a rate. A rate (`finance_fx_rates`) is either:

- **published** by the European Central Bank's euro foreign exchange reference rates (about 30 currencies against the euro, once a working day, `https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/eurofxref/eurofxref-daily.xml`), fetched by the `ecb_fx` connector and stored with the ECB's date, source name and address. The ECB publishes them for information and allows reuse with the source named; ANUM names the source and date on every rate. The UAE dirham is not in the ECB list: it is pegged by the Central Bank of the UAE at 3.6725 dirhams per US dollar, so the connector also stores EUR to AED as the ECB's EUR to USD rate times that peg, with the source saying so (`ECB EUR/USD x CBUAE peg 3.6725 AED per USD`);
- **typed** by the person (`POST /fx-rates`, source `manual`), with the date they give (within the last year, never in the future).

The ECB answer is untrusted internet content: it is parsed by a strict pattern (never an XML parser that could expand entities), capped at 64 KB, every code must be three letters and listed once, every rate a number between 0.000001 and 10,000,000, the date within the last 10 days and not in the future; anything else is refused and the connection shows the error. Conversions use the newest rate within the age limit (a published rate wins over a typed one on the same date): direct, inverse, or a cross through a shared base. A converted figure is rounded once, half to even, to the target currency's minor unit.

## Guardian

Stage 4 (`finance/guardian.py`, `finance/policy.py`). The person's own spending and risk rules, stored per person and versioned, and a pure, deterministic evaluator:

    evaluate(action, rules, ledger_state) -> Decision(allow | needs_approval | deny, reasons)

**Rules** (`GuardianRules`; empty means "no rule of this kind"): allowed currencies; a single payment limit and a daily total limit per currency; monthly caps per category and currency; blocked categories; blocked merchants (whole words or phrases of the cleaned description); "your approval at or above" an amount per currency; quiet hours (local time, may wrap past midnight); a cooling-off period (hours) for amounts at or above a threshold per currency. One limit per currency (and per category for caps); amounts follow the money rules. Money lent to or borrowed from a person, and its repayments (a transaction a live `person_loan` link names, [Lent and borrowed](#lent-and-borrowed)), is a transfer for the Guardian as for the summary, the advisor and budgets: it never counts towards a cap, a daily total or an alert, is never in the review queue, and saving or editing it is not checked (`obligations.lending_ids_on` reads the links on the Guardian's own finance unit of work; tests `test_finance_personal_money.py::test_the_guardians_caps_treat_lent_and_borrowed_money_as_a_transfer`, `test_postgres_finance_personal_money.py`).

**Evaluation.** The same inputs always give the same decision; nothing reads a clock, a store or the network. Reasons come in a fixed order, each with a code, an outcome, a message, the rule and parameters. Deny: an invalid amount or time, a currency not allowed, a blocked category or merchant, over the single limit, over the daily total (today's spend plus this amount), over a monthly cap (this month's spend in the category plus this amount). Needs approval: at or above the approval amount, within quiet hours, within the cooling-off period (until first asked plus the hours), or asked by an assistant for a money-moving action. The outcome is the most severe reason. A `record` (writing down money already gone) is checked against the limits and blocks only: approval and the time rules do not apply to it.

**The gate.** `gate(action, rules, state)` is the mandatory check every finance action passes: the rules plus the kernel's own limits, which no rule can lift. Today the first kernel limit is absolute: ANUM moves no money, so every `spend`, `bill_payment`, `transfer` and `trade` is denied (`execution_not_available`). The execution gateway (stage 7) calls `gate` for every request and acts only on `allow`, or on `needs_approval` once the person approved that exact request; for a SIMULATED paper or shadow order (`gate(..., simulated=True)`) the kernel adds only the note `simulated_only` and the rules decide, so the person sees which rules would block the real action.

**Where it is used now.** Creating or changing a transaction returns `guardian` (what the rules say about that record; it is saved anyway, the money is gone) and the web names any rule it breaks; a confirmed import returns `rule_breaks`; `GET /guardian/alerts?month=` lists caps reached or within 20 percent, payments to blocked merchants or categories, days over the daily total and payments over the single limit (uncategorized money out is watched too: it may be spending, so a big unknown debit is flagged before the person files it); `POST /guardian/evaluate` is a dry run of any action ("Check a spend"), returning the rules' verdict and the gate's, and noting that nothing was paid, sent or saved.

**Pausing a rule.** `paused` names kinds of rule the person set but does not want applied for now (for example `["max_single"]`). A paused kind is kept as written and resumed with one change; while paused, `evaluate` (and so `gate`, the alerts, the review queue and import rule breaks) leaves it out. The kernel's own limits are never paused: ANUM still moves no money. Only kinds that are set can be paused (others are dropped). Pausing is a change like any other (proposed, then confirmed with its payload hash) and it **loosens** the kind, so an assistant can never pause a rule; raising a paused limit is also loosening, checked on the rules as written. `paused` is left out of the payload hash while empty, so rules saved before pausing existed keep their hash. Pausing every kind is how the page's "Pause monitoring" works: the rules stay, nothing is flagged until they are resumed.

**Changing rules.** Every change, the person's own included, is a proposal (`POST /guardian/changes`) on the current version, with a SHA-256 payload hash over the change id, the owner, the base version and the exact rules (the same binding rule as approvals, `approval_integrity.py`). It takes effect only when the person confirms it with the hash they were shown (`POST /guardian/changes/{id}/confirm {"payload_hash": ...}`): a different hash, a change that lapsed (24 hours) or one made on an older version is refused with 409 and nothing changes; confirming adds a new version (unique per person, so two confirmations cannot both land) and supersedes every other pending change. The web shows the new rules, what is new, what is no longer a rule, what the change loosens and the first characters of the code before the person confirms. Every proposal, confirmation and rejection is audited with ids, version, rule-kind codes and who proposed it, never a limit. **Loosening** (`loosening(old, new)`: a limit raised or removed, a block removed, quiet hours narrowed, a shorter cooling-off) is listed on the change; an assistant's proposal that loosens anything is refused, by the code and by a database check.

### Review queue

`finance/guardian_review.py`, migration `0140_finance_guardian_reviews`. Guardian is a monitoring aid: for one month (`GET /guardian/review?month=&state=awaiting|resolved|snoozed|all&limit=&offset=`) it checks every payment out of the person's ledger against the rules that apply now, as `evaluate` checks a `record`, in posting order (each payment against the month's and the day's spend before it). Every payment a rule flags is an item with the rules' reasons, the rule kinds, the account, and what is typical: the median of the person's earlier payments (up to a year back) with the same cleaned description and currency, and how many there were. The answer also counts the month's flagged, awaiting, resolved and snoozed items and, per rule kind that is set, how many payments it flagged and the last date. Approval, quiet hours and cooling off apply to an action asked for (a paper or live order, a dry run), never to money already recorded, so they never flag a payment.

`POST /guardian/review/{transaction_id} {"decision": "expected" | "reviewed" | "snoozed" | "reopen", "snooze_days": 1 | 3 | 7 | 30}` records the person's decision (`finance:manage`): one row per person and payment (`finance_guardian_reviews`, forced RLS with the owner-only restrictive policy and the purge policy), with the amount and currency it was decided on, so a corrected payment waits for review again. A snooze lasts until the start of the day that many days ahead in the person's time zone; `reopen` ends the decision. A decision is a note only: nothing is paid, held, approved or sent to a bank. It is audited as `finance.guardian.review` with the transaction id, the decision and rule codes, never an amount or a description; the decisions are in the privacy export (`guardian_reviews`) and deleted with the person's finance data.

`GET /guardian/activity?limit=&cursor=` is the person's own Guardian audit trail (`finance.guardian.*` records whose actor is the caller: proposals, confirmations, rejections and review decisions), newest first, keyset paged. Ids, codes and versions only. The workspace audit log stays owner only.

### Notifications

Guardian does not send notifications today. Its alerts and the review queue are shown in Finance, Guardian (and the Safe to spend preview); there is no bell notification, e-mail or SMS for them. ANUM sends no e-mail or SMS on its own, so the page shows e-mail and SMS delivery as not available with that reason. Guardian's quiet hours are a rule on actions (an action asked for in that window needs approval), not a notification setting; the page shows them with the time zone from Finance settings.

## Connectors

Stage 5 (`finance/connectors.py`, `finance/sync.py`). Read-only sources that bring outside data into the person's own ledger.

- **Registry** (`GET /connectors`): each source with its kind, read-only scopes, whether it needs credentials, sync interval, terms, and for a source that is not built, what the owner must set up first. Only sources with a built adapter can be connected.
- **Built:** `ecb_fx`, the ECB reference rates ([Exchange rates](#exchange-rates)): no account, no key, every 12 hours.
- **Listed, not built (owner actions):** bank and card accounts through a UAE Open Finance (Al Tareq) licensed provider, a broker (positions and cash), a crypto exchange (balances), market prices. Each needs a provider account and keys ANUM does not have and an adapter checked against the provider's documented API; they cannot be connected and nothing about them is faked.
- **Connections** (`GET, POST /connections`, `GET, PATCH /connections/{id}`): one per person and provider (a source without credentials is connected once). Credentials are write only: validated against the source's fields, Fernet-encrypted with `ANUM_SECRETS_KEY` at once, never returned (only `has_credentials` and a short hint), re-encrypted by `python -m anum_api.rotate_secrets` in each owner's own scope, and never exported.
- **Health:** status (`active`, `error`, `disconnected`), last sync, last success, next sync, the last error as a safe message, and the last counts.
- **Sync now** (`POST /connections/{id}/sync`): runs one sync; **disconnect** (`POST /connections/{id}/disconnect`) stops syncing and erases the credentials (a database check keeps a disconnected connection without them), data already fetched stays; **delete** removes the connection, its credentials and the rates it fetched (transactions it imported stay in the ledger, undoable as an import).
- **Imported transactions** land in the stage 2 ledger only for accounts the connection maps, with the same dedupe hash (keyed by the provider's own id), cleaned like statement text and categorized by the same rules, so a repeated sync adds nothing twice.

### Sync

A sync never holds a database transaction across the network call: it reads the connection in one short unit of work in the owner's scope, fetches with none open (one GET, no redirects, a 256 KB cap, a 15-second timeout), then writes the results and the connection's health in a second unit of work. A failure is recorded on the connection and retried after `ANUM_FINANCE_RETRY_MINUTES`. With `ANUM_FINANCE_SYNC_ENABLED=true` every API process runs the background loop: every `ANUM_FINANCE_SYNC_POLL_SECONDS` it finds due connections as `anum_maintenance` (id, scope, owner, `enabled`, `status`, `next_sync_at` only), claims each with a compare-and-set update of `next_sync_at` and syncs it as the application role in the owner's own tenant, workspace and user scope ([Multi-tenancy](multi-tenancy.md#maintenance-role)). The API login needs `anum_maintenance` granted `WITH INHERIT FALSE, SET TRUE`.

## Assistant

Every finance tool is `caller_scoped`: a task whose run plans one becomes a [private task](tasks.md#private-tasks) of its creator before the tool runs, so the answer ("Balances: AED ...") is stored where only that person reads it, whether it was asked in Tasks, in the chat or by an automation, and another person's run of the same task is refused. Before November 2026 such a task and its runs were readable by every member with `task:read`, viewers included; migration `0163_private_tasks` hides the existing ones it can identify (a run that proposed a `finance.*` tool).

Read-only tools (`finance/tools.py`, registered in the API's and the Temporal worker's registries by `register_finance_tools`), each with an `input_schema`, `action_kind="read"` and `RiskLevel.LOW`, on the caller's own ledger only:

- `finance.summary` (`month`)
- `finance.transactions.search` (`query`, `category`, `from`, `to`, `direction`, `limit`)
- `finance.bills.upcoming` (`days`)
- `finance.safe_to_spend` (no arguments): the figure per currency, line totals, the per-day figure and, when on, the combined total; always "an estimate, not advice"
- `finance.guardian.rules`: the rules in plain words, the version, this month's alerts and how many changes wait for the person
- `finance.guardian.check` (`amount`, `currency`, `category`, `merchant`, `kind`): a dry run through `gate`, as an assistant's request (so a money-moving kind always needs the person); it says nothing was done

One tool writes, and only a proposal: `finance.guardian.propose` (`action_kind="create"`, `RiskLevel.LOW`, `edit`: a category cap, a blocked merchant or category, a single or daily limit, an approval amount). It applies the edit to the current rules and stores a pending change marked `agent`; a change that would loosen anything is refused ("Only you can loosen your own rules"). There is no tool that confirms, rejects or deletes rules, connects a source, reads a credential, or pays, transfers or trades: confirming is only the person's own `POST .../confirm` with the hash they were shown.

The suggestion queue adds `finance.suggestions` (read: what waits for review, with the evidence and confidence) and `finance.rules.propose` (`action_kind="create"`: a proposed categorisation rule with provenance `agent`, which runs only after the owner approves it with its payload hash under Finance, Suggestions, Rules). No tool accepts or rejects a suggestion, approves or applies a rule, undoes a batch or changes an automation policy ([Suggestions](#suggestions)).

Stages 6 to 9 add one read tool and two proposal tools: `finance.paper.portfolios` (read: the SIMULATED portfolios, cash, positions, P&L and the assumptions, "not real trades and no promise of returns"), `finance.paper.propose` (`action_kind="create"`: a paper or shadow trade request marked `agent`, checked by the Guardian gate, which adds "an assistant asked for this: you approve it yourself"; `mode: live` is refused) and `finance.live.widening.propose` (`action_kind="create"`: the next wider-live tier, refused while wider live use is off). No tool approves, executes, turns live on, sets live limits or changes a kill switch. The planner proposes a simulated order only when the user's own words say paper, simulated, shadow or تجريبي ("paper buy 10 EXMPL", "اشتر 10 EXMPL تجريبي"); "show my paper portfolio" and "المحفظة التجريبية" read the portfolios; any other buy, sell, pay or transfer still goes to `anum.external-action`. The skill's triggers add paper, shadow, simulate, simulated, simulation, portfolio and, in Arabic, تجريبي, تجريبيا, تجريبية, محاكاة, محفظة, المحفظة, محفظتي.

Fund research adds the read tool `finance.research` (`symbol`, or none for the owner's IBKR portfolio as IBKR last reported it): text built from the gathered data only, never a free model answer, so no number outside the data reaches the reply ([Fund research](finance-research.md#assistant)). "research SPUS" and "how is my portfolio doing" ask it. Not financial advice; no guaranteed returns.

The `anum.finance` skill (`agent_skills.py`) is selected by words such as spent, spending, budget, bills, balance, debts, transactions, afford, rules, cap, limit, block, guardian and, in Arabic, مصاريف, فواتير, رصيد, صرفت, ميزانية, ديون, أصرف, متاح, قواعد, حدود. Its planner (`finance.tools.plan_call`) reads only the user's own words (threat model G5): "what did I spend this month" asks `finance.summary`, "how much did I spend on groceries in September" and "transactions at Carrefour" search, "bills due this week" lists bills, "how much can I spend this month" and "كم أقدر أصرف هذا الشهر" ask `finance.safe_to_spend`, "show my spending rules" and "ما هي قواعد الصرف" read the rules, "cap dining at 600", "block payments to talabat", "never spend more than 2000 at once" and "no more than 300 a day" propose a stricter rule. Pay, transfer, send, buy, sell and "spend 200 on ..." return nothing and fall through to `anum.external-action`.

Tool output is untrusted data (descriptions come from statements): in chat it reaches the model only through `tool_output_prompt_block` after the untrusted-data rules (`chat_tools.summary_messages`, category `finance`), and voice grounding labels it the same way. The output carries a note that descriptions are data, not instructions.

## Paper and shadow trading

Stage 6 (`finance/trading.py`). **SIMULATED**: nothing is bought, sold, paid or sent, and no real account is used. Every screen, tool answer and result says so, with the assumptions used, and says nothing certain about what a real order would earn or lose.

- **Portfolios.** A **paper** portfolio has simulated starting cash per currency; a **shadow** portfolio has no cash and records what each order would have done. Up to 20 per person. Each has its own fee (basis points of the order's value, with a minimum in the order's currency), slippage (basis points against the order: a buy pays more, a sell gets less) and the oldest price it uses (1 to 30 days, default 3). New costs apply to later fills only.
- **Prices, never invented.** A security fills only at the newest price the person recorded for that symbol and currency (`POST /prices`, source `manual`, within the last year, never a future price) within the portfolio's age limit. A currency (instrument `fx`) fills at the newest recorded exchange rate (stage 5), direct, inverse or across a shared base. With no price a **market** order is refused with the reason ("record a price, or place a limit order"); a **limit** order waits, and is tried again whenever a price is recorded or on **Check waiting orders**.
- **Fills.** Fill price = recorded price moved by the slippage; value = quantity x fill price, rounded once to the currency; fee = the larger of the fee rate and the minimum. A limit buy fills only at or below its limit, a sell at or above. Paper checks the simulated cash (value plus fee) and units held (no short selling) at fill time; shadow has no cash and never refuses for it.
- **Book.** Cash, positions and P&L are replayed from the filled orders of the current run, average cost: realised P&L on each sale (proceeds after fee minus the cost of the units sold), unrealised P&L at the newest recorded price (a position without a recent price is listed as not valued). Fees and slippage paid are shown per currency. **Reset** starts a new run (cash back to the start or new starting cash, waiting orders cancelled); earlier runs stay in the history.
- **Shadow comparison.** Each filled shadow order against the newest recorded price: a buy gains when the price rose, a sell when it fell, fees taken off, with how many days ago it was placed and a total per currency.
- **Agents** may only propose paper or shadow orders; the person approves them like their own.

## Execution gateway

Stage 7 (`finance/gateway.py`). The one governed path for any finance action that would move money or trade. Every request goes through it in this order and stops at the first refusal:

1. **Intent.** `POST /execution/intents` with the mode, the exact request and an optional idempotency key (the same key returns the same request; a different request with it is refused). A `live` request is refused here, before anything else.
2. **Kill switches.** The organisation's or the person's switch stops every executor, simulated ones too.
3. **Guardian.** The same `gate` as stage 4, on the order's value (estimate from the recorded price, or the limit), with the symbol as the merchant; for paper and shadow the kernel adds only `simulated_only`, so the person sees exactly which of their rules would block the real action. **Deny stops**: the request is stored as `denied` with the reasons and cannot be approved. A cooling-off reason keeps the approval open past its end.
4. **Approval bound to the payload hash.** The same binding rule as ANUM's approvals (`approval_integrity.py`): SHA-256 of the canonical JSON of the id, owner, mode, kind, portfolio, instrument, currency, side, order type, quantity, limit, amount, account and idempotency key. The person approves with the hash they were shown; the gateway recomputes it from the stored request, so any change invalidates it. It expires after `ANUM_FINANCE_APPROVAL_MINUTES` (default 30), is used once (a compare-and-set claim from `pending_approval` to `executing`), only the owner can approve (the store, and a database check on the approver), and there is no agent tool for it. At approval the kill switches and the Guardian gate are checked again with the current rules and price: a rule added since blocks it. Finance approvals live in the owner-only `finance_exec_intents` table rather than the workspace approvals queue, because that queue is routed to workspace approvers and would show one person's private finance to others.
5. **Executor.** `paper` and `shadow` (stage 6) are the only executors. The request id is the idempotency key: one simulated order per request, ever (unique in the database).
6. **Result.** Stored on the request and audited (`finance.exec.intent.create`, `.approve`, `.reject`, `.refused`, with modes, statuses and rule codes only; refusals with `outcome: denied`).

**Kill switches** (`PUT /execution/kill-switch`): the person's own, and the organisation's (owners and organisation administrators). Both audited (`finance.exec.kill_switch.person`, `.global`).

## Limited live

Everything below describes the generic path, which still refuses every live request.

- The person's **live limits** (`LiveLimits`: at most per action, per day and per month per currency, allowed currencies, instruments and accounts, a cooling-off of 1 to 168 hours; empty means nothing is allowed) change only through a request of mode `settings` that the person approves with its payload hash (`POST /execution/live/changes`). **Opting in** needs the same approval and is refused while live is not enabled; **opting out** is immediate. `check_live_limits` is the pure check a live executor would have to pass.

## Wider live

Stage 9 (`finance/wider.py`, `finance/wider_api.py`): **the framework only, shipped off.** Wider live use could only follow a limited stage that has proved itself; nothing here runs live.

- `ANUM_FINANCE_WIDER_LIVE_ENABLED` defaults to false and is refused unless `ANUM_FINANCE_LIVE_ENABLED` is on (itself refused); the Helm chart refuses it too. While it is off every proposal and approval is refused ("Wider live use is not enabled").
- **Graduation** (`GET /execution/wider`), computed on the server from the stage 7 and 8 records over `ANUM_FINANCE_WIDER_WINDOW_DAYS` (default 30): every stage 8 precondition met, at least `ANUM_FINANCE_WIDER_MIN_DAYS` (30) since the live opt-in, at least `ANUM_FINANCE_WIDER_MIN_ACTIONS` (20) executed live actions, no Guardian denial overridden, no failed execution or reconciliation, no kill switch turned on, not frozen. Shown as a checklist with each value and what is needed. Today it is never met (no live action can run).
- **Widening** is a proposal by the person (`POST /execution/wider/widenings`) or an assistant (`finance.live.widening.propose`) to move **exactly one tier** up: each limit at most `ANUM_FINANCE_WIDER_STEP_PERCENT` (50) percent of the stage 8 limits above the last tier, at most `ANUM_FINANCE_WIDER_MAX_TIER` (3, hard maximum 5) tiers, no new currency, no shorter cooling-off, at most one new action type and none removed. The owner approves it with its payload hash, once, within 24 hours (`.../approve`); it takes effect only after `ANUM_FINANCE_WIDER_COOLING_HOURS` (72). An assistant can never approve, and a widening never changes the Guardian rules: loosening a rule is only ever the person's own Guardian change, reviewed with its code.
- **Automatic step down.** A kill switch turned on, a failed execution, or three Guardian denials within a day is an incident: the tier drops one step, an approved step still cooling off is cancelled, and widening is frozen until the owner reviews it (`POST /execution/wider/review`). Incidents are checked whenever wider live use is read, proposed or decided, and audited (`finance.wider.step_down`).
- The execution gateway still refuses every live request whatever the tier (tested with both flags forced on and a tier above zero).

## Installment statement import, reimport, and purchase dashboard

Reimporting the same file leaves existing source history unchanged. A changed amount or due date is reported as a conflict and the prior financial terms are retained until reviewed. One exception: the same installment and due date with a **lower** amount is Tamara's own new schedule after a refund. It is not a conflict: the original amount stays, the lower figure is kept on the row (`reported_amount`) and listed in the result's `refund_signals`, and [Tamara refunds](#tamara-refunds) applies it on the owner's confirmation. A refund the owner applied (`refunded_amount`) survives every reimport, and an upload can never set either field. Source-status changes are saved. An upload that lists the same provider order installment twice, or an installment number above the plan length, is refused as a whole (422) and nothing is saved.
- **Bills are payment obligations only:** New scheduled installments become one-time Finance Bills. Existing bills are checked by source order and installment; they are not duplicated. CSV-reported paid or refund-review rows do not become new payable bills. If a previously scheduled existing bill is subsequently reported paid or under review, the importer pauses it instead of fabricating a bank payment. Human-paused bills remain paused.
- **Verified versus provider-reported payment:** The full summary includes all purchases, installment counts, source-reported paid amounts, scheduled outstanding and refund-review amounts. Independently **verified** payment matches are shown separately and may override the provider status for displayed outstanding calculations. Only the Finance ledger's matched payment links count as verified bank payments.
- **Export:** the statement CSV writes each amount still due exactly, with the currency's own decimals (KWD three, JPY none), from the same thousandths arithmetic as the rest of the web (`lib/financeRefunds.ts` `rowDueText`).
- **Source provenance:** Saved statement rows preserve the imported source email link and review notes. The full dashboard allows purchase search, completed/open/review filtering, order drill-down, monthly due totals and CSV export.
- **Money and privacy:** No import charges cards or creates canonical bank transactions, and no personal statement is committed to the repository. Source email links and notes belong only to the signed-in owner. Amounts under refund/dispute review are excluded from payable totals, and original statement amounts are not represented as confirmed refunds or invoice totals.

**Known limitations:** Provider email status may lag real payment activity. A final bank/Tamara reconciliation is needed for disputed refunds and reported paid amounts. The source CSV describes merchants/orders, not the actual item-level receipts, unless receipts are supplied separately. A new installment record may be due even if it is not yet matched to a real payment.


### Gmail receipts alongside manual and CSV import

Finance > Bills now presents **Sync Gmail**, **Import CSV**, and **Add Bill** independently. Manual Bills are never deleted when Gmail is connected or disconnected. Gmail integration reuses the ANUM Email module's existing Gmail OAuth connection (ANUM does not reuse the ChatGPT Gmail connection).

1. Connect the intended Gmail account in **ANUM Email**, preferably as **Private**. Finance prefers `shahzeb.rahmanllc@gmail.com` when that mailbox is among the authenticated user's own connections; it never assumes this Gmail identity is already authorized.
2. In Finance > Bills > **Sync Gmail**, select the mailbox, refresh ANUM Email's existing incremental sync, and preview Tamara messages.
3. Finance's read-only preview endpoint `GET /api/v1/finance/installment-email/preview?account_id=...&limit=500` only allows Gmail mailboxes connected by the current user, requires both finance manage and email read permissions, reads relevant metadata from the existing inbox, and fetches bodies transiently through the provider. It does not send, archive, or mark mail read. Its mailbox and message reads run in a worker thread in their own short unit of work, never on the event loop ([Event loop](realtime.md#event-loop)). The mailbox list is `GET /api/v1/finance/installment-email/mailboxes`.
4. Parsing is **deterministic** and limited to Tamara sender addresses, order numbers, original due schedules and repayment confirmations. It flags amount mismatches for human review. A refund or order-updated email that names an amount, or says the whole order was refunded or cancelled, is returned as refund evidence (`refunds` in the preview) and the schedule stays as Tamara sent it; approving the import saves that evidence with the rows (`refund_notices` on the sync) for [Tamara refunds](#tamara-refunds). A read that finds only refund emails and no new schedule rows can still be approved: the sync then takes `entries: []` with the notices (an empty sync with neither is refused, 422). A refund email with neither still sets the order's unpaid installments to review. No model tokens or LLM provider are required.
5. The user explicitly approves the preview before its rows are stored using the same idempotent Finance statement sync API and before any future scheduled Bills are created. Provider-reported paid installments are preserved in statement history but not fabricated as bank transactions. Refund review cannot be automatically treated as money received.
6. Provider replies, invoices and SKU-level product details cannot be guaranteed from an order-only email. Original invoices/receipts must be separately scanned if SKU-level detail is required.
7. The Email module's normal background mailbox sync continues under its own schedule, but **Finance does not silently import or modify bill records on a background timer**: approve the Finance import for each new batch. This prevents accidental financial obligations from unreviewed email content.

The scanner checks at most 500 locally synchronized messages per manual preview and warns if it hits the limit. Older invoices absent from the local Email cache are not claimed as scanned; use CSV or the existing Email archive/backfill process to cover them. None of these endpoints require or publish OAuth tokens or Gmail bodies in the Finance response.

### Tamara refunds

When a Tamara order is refunded in full or in part, the installments the refund cancelled must stop being due everywhere in ANUM. Finance > Bills > **Refunds** (above the installment statement) finds the refund evidence for every order, shows the new schedule and saves it only when the owner presses **Apply refunds** and confirms. Nothing here moves money, marks a payment as received, or changes an email.

**Evidence, searched in three places and linked to its order** (`anum_api/finance/installment_refunds.py`):

| Source | Strong (can be applied) | Weak (Needs review, never applied) |
|---|---|---|
| Tamara refund or order-updated emails kept from an approved Gmail import (`finance_plans.installment_refund_notices`) | An email naming the order number and an amount, or saying the whole order was refunded or cancelled. Two emails with the same amount within 3 days are one refund, counted once | An email without an amount |
| The owner's imported Tamara statement | A row the statement marks refunded or cancelled (CSV status `refunded`, `cancelled`, `void`), or a lower amount a reimport reported for the same installment and due date | A review row whose notes mention a refund |
| Money-in transactions in the ledger that name Tamara | A credit naming the order number; a credit matching a refund email of that order (within 3 days before to 45 days after it), by its amount or as the paid share coming back (up to what was paid), which is shown as **money back received**, not as a second refund, even when it also names the order | A credit naming only the merchant, or matching only an amount, without the order number |

Each order lists the evidence used, its strength and a link back to the email (owner only).

**The arithmetic.** Tamara's rule is not the same for every merchant and region: Tamara's [Key Fact Statement](https://tamara.co/en-ae/key-fact-statement) says a refund smaller than the outstanding balance is applied proportionally to the upcoming installments; Amazon.ae and Amazon.sa's Tamara help shows a different example (an AED 1,200 plan with an AED 500 return keeps the 2nd installment and cancels the 3rd and 4th; a return equal to what is outstanding completes the plan; a larger one cancels the rest and the excess goes back to the card); and some merchants recalculate an unpaid plan to the new order value. So ANUM never guesses one:

1. **Tamara's own data wins.** When the statement (or a reimport) shows the new schedule, refunded rows or lower amounts, exactly that is applied. It is checked first, so a refund email whose amount covers everything unpaid never cancels installments the revised statement still shows as due (the difference is listed as a reason).
2. **Certain without a schedule:** a full refund, or refunds that add up to at least what was still unpaid, cancel every unpaid installment. Paid installments stay paid; the rest of the refund, up to what was paid, is **money back to your card**, shown as expected (and as received when a matching ledger credit exists). These can be applied with the one-click confirmation.
3. **A partial refund with no new schedule from Tamara** is shown as an **estimate** (the Key Fact Statement's proportional split over the upcoming installments, each share rounded to the minor unit and the last taking the remainder), labelled "confirm in the Tamara app", and the order stays in **Needs review**. An estimate is never applied, and never marks one installment cancelled.

Amounts are exact `Decimal` in the currency's minor unit; refunds in another currency are ignored. Refunds that add up to more than the order total, or less than what is already applied, put the order in Needs review and cannot be applied.

**Order status:** Active (no refund), Partly refunded (some still due), Refunded (nothing left to pay), Needs review (weak evidence or an estimate). The installment statement shows the same status per order, a Refunded filter and the refunded amount per installment and currency.

**Saving.** `GET /api/v1/finance/installment-refunds` returns every order's plan (evidence, old and new amount per installment, outstanding and next due before and after, money back, reasons) with a `plan_hash`. `POST /api/v1/finance/installment-refunds/apply` takes the orders and the hashes the page showed: a changed plan answers 409 (reload and check again), an order with nothing strong to apply 422, and the request is all or nothing. Applying records `refunded_amount` on each statement row (the original `amount` stays) and changes the one-time bills imported for those installments (matched by `Order:` and `Installment:` in the bill's notes and the same due date): a fully refunded installment's bill is paused, a partly refunded one is lowered to what is still due. Each apply is kept as a record (`finance_plans.installment_refunds`, migration `0146_installment_refunds`) with what changed, and `POST /api/v1/finance/installment-refunds/{id}/undo` puts the latest one of an order back exactly (a bill the owner changed since is left as they set it). Both are audited (`finance.installment_refunds.apply`, `.undo`) with counts only, never amounts or names. Reading needs finance read; applying and undoing need finance manage; each person sees only their own statement (owner RLS on `finance_plans`). The routes are plain `def` routes, so the database work runs in the thread pool, never on the event loop ([Event loop](realtime.md#event-loop)).

**Downstream.** A paused bill is not due anywhere and a lowered bill is due at its new amount: bills due and upcoming bills (`/summary`, `/bill-schedule`), Safe to spend, the money advisor's coming payments, and the installment statement's outstanding, next due, monthly and overdue totals all exclude the refunded part (tested in `services/api/tests/test_finance_installment_refunds.py`).

**Not done by design:** ANUM does not read the Tamara app or its API (no public API for customers), so a partial refund without an updated statement or email schedule stays an estimate until the owner imports Tamara's new figures.


## Multi-currency bank/card statement import: USD purchase and SAR posting

Finance > Import supports both CSV and reviewed PDF bank statements with international card charges. **The account ledger always records the amount and currency actually billed by the bank.** For example, a USD 20.00 purchase with SAR 76.80 billed becomes one SAR 76.80 debit (signed `-76.80`) and retains the original USD 20.00, bank FX rate and fee in its notes. It does not create a second USD withdrawal or use an estimated FX rate.

- CSV optional column mapping: *transaction currency, original/foreign amount, original currency, bank-billed amount, billed currency, exchange rate, FX/transaction fee*. Common header variants are detected automatically and each one may be manually mapped. Original amounts, actual settlement and bank-stated fee/rate appear in the preview and survive import in the transaction's notes.
- The account's actual ledger currency governs the posted amount. A foreign currency row with no bank-billed account-currency amount is flagged in the preview as a row error, not silently converted, and other valid rows can still be imported. The user can map the billed amount column or select an account denominated in the original currency.
- PDF statement review shows a separate bank-billed amount input for rows in a currency other than the selected account. The user must supply the exact signed account-currency amount before including the row; debit versus credit direction must match. PDF balance checks use the billed amount.
- If a PDF reader already extracts the amount in the account currency and shows the foreign purchase in the `original_amount` / `original_currency` columns, the bank-billed account amount is preserved as-is. A printed FX fee or VAT on the **same transaction** remains informational to avoid counting it twice. **A separately posted fee line** is imported as its own `fees` expense.
- The rules categorize positive `cashback` and `refunds` separately from other `income`. International transaction charges, FX markup, interest and annual fees take priority over generic merchant descriptions. Existing owner-created category rules still take precedence, and manual categories are not replaced.
- CSV/PDF duplicate protection remains based on date, posted account-currency amount and cleaned merchant/description. Reuploading the same file will not double post transactions.
- Three-letter ISO currency codes use the applicable minor units (e.g. KWD three decimal places); no fabricated FX rates or currency conversion is performed.

**Scope:** These safeguards apply to new reviewed imports. Previously imported misclassified transactions are not silently rewritten. Review the transaction's import preview and the bank's own original and billed figures before confirming. The existing Finance PDF source parser may require layout mapping or OCR corrections on unusual bank statement formats.


## Personal expense learning and corrected rent direction

- **Room Rent** (`room_rent`) and **Food and meals** (`food`) are first-class expense categories alongside existing Housing, Dining and Groceries. Common Arabic/English descriptions and Saudi merchants are recognized for new records.
- In Finance > Transactions, choose a category, optionally tick **Remember this category for future matching descriptions**, and review a specific merchant/description phrase. The backend stores an owner-scoped Finance category rule (a versioned `learned` rule once the owner has [rules](#rules-and-learning)), which is applied to future bank statement previews, CSV/PDF imports and manually recorded transactions. It does **not** silently rewrite historical transactions or learn from another user's data: earlier rows change only through **Apply to similar** with a preview and an undo. Generic phrases such as `rent`, `payment`, `card` and `food` are rejected as overbroad.
- A **positive** amount with a description indicating room rent is flagged for review in CSV import preview and the transaction details drawer. No positive amount is automatically changed to a negative value. For a manual or statement-origin transaction, the drawer offers an explicitly confirmed **Correct to money out** action that changes the sign through the normal finance transaction PATCH and canonical journal repost. For SMS/Apple Pay-origin transactions, correct their payment through Finance > iPhone SMS > Review.
- If you paid SAR 2,600 for room rent but it appears as +SAR 2,600, inspect the actual bank statement and correct the specific entry to -SAR 2,600 with the Room Rent category. The application must not infer a specific transaction ID or make an automatic correction based solely on amount or text.
- The monthly Finance summary separates income, cashback, merchant refunds, expenses and net cash flow. Cashbacks and refunds do not inflate earned income; confirmed positive credits remain part of actual net cash movement.
- Previously imported records are never silently recategorized or reversed. The audit trail, source import fingerprint and ledger correction rules remain intact. Reimporting the original statement after a human correction does not double count that same bank entry.

- **Bank DR/CR column:** CSV statement mapping supports a separate DR/CR or Debit/Credit direction column. Explicit DR converts a positive unsigned number to a ledger debit; CR means credit. Conflicts between the supplied sign and credit indicator are held as row errors. Descriptions alone never change the money direction. The selected bank account currency still determines the posted currency.

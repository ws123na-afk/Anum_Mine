# Calendar and next actions

The workspace calendar backs the ANUM 360 "Calendar & next actions" and "Meeting scheduled" screens (`docs/design/anum360.md`). It holds the workspace's events with their attendees and suggests next steps from the workspace's own state. The code is `services/api/anum_api/calendar_api.py`, `services/api/anum_api/db/calendar_repository.py`, `apps/web/src/CalendarView.tsx` and `apps/mobile/lib/features/calendar/`. Sync with Google Calendar and Microsoft 365 is `calendar_sync.py`, `calendar_providers.py`, `calendar_sync_api.py` and `db/calendar_sync_repository.py` ([Sync](#sync)).

This module is calendar events only. Meeting transcripts and summaries are a separate meetings module.

## Events

An event has a `title`, `kind` (`meeting`, `call`, `review`, `reminder`, `focus`), `status` (`scheduled`, `cancelled`), `starts_at` and `ends_at` (stored in UTC; a time without an offset is taken as UTC; at most 14 days long and ending after it starts), optional `description`, `location`, `conference_url` (https only; the app's Join opens it) and `phone` (the app's Call dials it), and up to 100 `attendees`. An attendee has a `name`, optional `email` (validated, unique within the event) and `role`, and the reply the organiser recorded: `invited`, `accepted`, `tentative` or `declined` (on a series, per occurrence where one was recorded: [Replies](#replies)). An event can also be all-day, repeat and carry reminders ([Recurrence](#recurrence), [All-day events](#all-day-events), [Reminders](#reminders)): `all_day`, `timezone`, `recurrence`, `reminders`, `email_reminders`.

## API

| Route | Who | Does |
|---|---|---|
| `GET /api/v1/calendar/events?start=&end=&include_cancelled=&limit=&offset=` | `calendar:read` (every role) | Events and the occurrences of series overlapping the range, earliest first. Default: today (UTC) and the next 7 days; at most 92 days; `limit` (at most 500) and `offset` page the list and `truncated` says when more follow; `can_write` tells the app whether to offer Add |
| `POST /api/v1/calendar/events` | `calendar:write` (members, owners) | Adds an event, a series or an all-day event |
| `GET /api/v1/calendar/events/{id}?occurrence_start=` | `calendar:read` | One event and `can_manage`; for a series, `occurrence_start` opens that occurrence (404 when it was deleted or never existed) |
| `PATCH /api/v1/calendar/events/{id}` | the creator (with `calendar:write`) or an owner (`calendar:manage`) | Changes fields, cancels (`status: cancelled`) or replaces the attendee list (existing attendee ids are kept). For a series `scope` (`all`, `this`, `following`) and `occurrence_start` say which occurrences change ([Recurrence](#recurrence)) |
| `PUT /api/v1/calendar/events/{id}/attendees/{attendee_id}/response` | the creator or an owner | Records an attendee's reply. On a series `{"scope": "this", "occurrence_start": ...}` records it for that occurrence only; `scope: all` (the default) for the series ([Replies](#replies)) |
| `POST /api/v1/calendar/events/{id}/sync/retry` | the creator or an owner | Sends an event waiting for its connected calendar again and starts its failure count again (409 when nothing waits or the calendar needs reconnecting) |
| `DELETE /api/v1/calendar/events/{id}?scope=&occurrence_start=` | the creator or an owner | Deletes it; for a series `scope` removes one occurrence, this and the following ones, or the whole series |
| `GET /api/v1/calendar/next-steps` | `calendar:read` | Suggestions, no model call: prepare for the next meetings in 24 hours, follow up with attendees who have not replied, send a summary of a meeting that ended in the last 24 hours (these three start a task, so they need `task:create`), reply to urgent unread email (with `email:read`) and review pending approvals (with `approval:read`) |

A task suggestion carries the task's `title` and `prompt`; the app creates it with `POST /api/v1/tasks` and opens it, so it runs and asks for approval like any other task. Event notes go into the prompt as data, not instructions.

Changes are audited (`calendar.event.created`, `calendar.event.updated` with the `scope`, `calendar.attendee.response` with the `scope` and the occurrence, `calendar.event.deleted` with the `scope`, `calendar.event.sync_retried`, `calendar.reminder_sent`, `calendar.reminder_emailed`).

An event also carries its sync link (`connection_id`, `external_id`, `synced_at`, `sync_state`), `sync_error` and `sync_failed` (a send that failed, and whether ANUM stopped retrying it) and `invitations_pending`; a create takes `sync` (default `true`; `false` keeps the event in ANUM only). See [Sync](#sync).

## Recurrence

A repeating event is one `calendar_events` row (the series) with a `recurrence` rule, and nothing else is stored for it: `GET /events` computes the occurrences inside the asked window (`anum_api/calendar_recurrence.py`, no third-party library; the subset is small enough to keep in one file with its own tests), at most 1000 per series per window and bounded by `limit`. The rule is the RFC 5545 RRULE subset ANUM supports, as a JSON object (the view also carries it as `rrule` text, for example `FREQ=WEEKLY;INTERVAL=2;BYDAY=MO,WE;UNTIL=20261231`):

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `freq` | `daily`, `weekly`, `monthly` or `yearly` |
| `interval` | every N days, weeks, months or years (1 to 99) |
| `by_weekday` | weekly: the weekdays (`MO` to `SU`; the start's weekday when empty). Monthly with `by_setpos`: the one weekday |
| `by_monthday` | monthly: the day of the month (1 to 31; `-1` the last day); months without that day are skipped |
| `by_setpos` | monthly: the nth weekday (1 to 4, `-1` the last) with one `by_weekday`, for "the second Tuesday" |
| `until` | the last day (in the series time zone) an occurrence may start on |
| `count` | how many occurrences (1 to 730); never with `until` |

A yearly series keeps its month and day (29 February happens in leap years only). The first occurrence is always the event's own start, even when the rule names other days. Occurrences keep the series' wall-clock time in its `timezone` (the request's, else the creator's profile zone, the workspace's first office, then `ANUM_DEFAULT_TIME_ZONE`), so a 09:00 meeting in Dubai stays at 09:00 across a clock change elsewhere; the stored `recurrence_until` (when the last occurrence ends, null for a series without an end) lets the store find the series that reach a window.

Each occurrence in `GET /events` is the series' fields with that occurrence's `starts_at` and `ends_at`, plus `occurrence_start` (its original start, the key of every edit or deletion of one occurrence), `occurrence_key` (tells occurrences apart), `is_recurring`, `overridden` and `override_id`. Exceptions:

* **Delete this occurrence** (`DELETE ...?scope=this&occurrence_start=`): the original start goes into the series' `skipped` list (at most 1000) and the occurrence is gone; its reminders are not sent.
* **Change this occurrence** (`PATCH` with `scope: this`): a child row (`series_id`, `recurrence_start`) replaces the computed occurrence with its own title, time, people, status or reminders (editing it again updates the same row); the view shows it `overridden`. Cancelling one occurrence is such a row with `status: cancelled`. A child row goes with its series when the series is deleted.
* **This and following** (`scope: following`): the series ends before the occurrence (`until` the day before, or `count` cut to the occurrences before it) and a new series starts there with the changes and the rest of the count. Exceptions after the split move to the new series when the time and rule did not change, otherwise they are dropped. From the first occurrence this is the same as `all`.
* **All** (`scope: all`, the default): the series itself. A new `starts_at` given with an `occurrence_start` moves every occurrence by the same amount; when the series' start or rule changes, its skipped list and changed occurrences are dropped (they no longer line up with it).

An occurrence that is not in the series (never generated, or skipped) answers 404 `That occurrence does not exist in this series`. Permission is the series': its creator (with `calendar:write`) or an owner.

## All-day events

`all_day: true` with `start_date` and `end_date` (inclusive, the same day when omitted) in `timezone` (resolved as above when empty): the stored `starts_at` and `ends_at` are the local midnights around those days, so range queries keep working and a timed instant given instead snaps to them. The view returns `start_date` and `end_date` again. A series can be all-day. The web shows them in a row above the hours (day and week) and without a time (month, agenda); they never count as double bookings.

## Replies

An attendee's reply on a series is the default for every occurrence. The organiser can record a reply for one occurrence (`PUT .../response` with `scope: this` and the occurrence's `occurrence_start`): it is kept in the series' `occurrence_responses` (at most 2000, `[{attendee_id, occurrence_start, response}]`) and shown on that occurrence only; the view lists such attendees in `occurrence_replies`. A changed occurrence (a child row) holds its own people, so a reply to it goes on its row, and when an occurrence is changed on its own its replies move onto that row. A reply with `scope: all` sets the series reply and clears the attendee's replies to single occurrences (a changed occurrence keeps the reply on its own row). Replies to an occurrence go with it when it is removed, with the attendee when they are taken off the list, and all of them when the series' start or rule changes; this and following keeps the earlier ones on the old series and moves the later ones to the new one. Provider replies arrive per instance: a Google instance an attendee answered on its own comes as a changed occurrence.

## Reminders

`reminders` holds up to five minutes-before values (0 = at the start, at most 40320 = four weeks), stored on the event and copied onto a changed occurrence (which may then have its own). When one falls due the event's creator and every workspace member whose team profile email is on the attendee list get an in-app notification (kind `calendar_reminder`: the title and when it starts, where, and how to join) and a phone push (`PushKind.CALENDAR_REMINDER`, fixed text in English or Arabic, opening the Calendar). A reminder whose time passed while the job was not running is still sent as long as the occurrence has not started; one for an occurrence under way is not; skipped and cancelled occurrences and cancelled events get none.

The job (`anum_api/calendar_reminders.py`) runs with the team reminder sweep (`team_work.run_reminder_pass`, `ANUM_TEAM_REMINDER_POLL_SECONDS`). With PostgreSQL candidate events (scheduled, with reminders, starting within four weeks or a running series) are discovered as `anum_maintenance` (ids, times, the rule and the reminder minutes: never a title or a person) and each is handled in its own transaction inside its tenant's RLS context. Every send is recorded in `calendar_reminder_sends` (event, occurrence, minutes, person), claimed with `insert ... on conflict do nothing`, so a pass that runs twice, after a restart or in two API processes never sends a reminder twice. Audited as `calendar.reminder_sent` (occurrence and recipient count only).

**Email reminders.** `email_reminders` names which of the reminders (minutes) also go by email (minutes that are not in `reminders` are dropped, so removing a reminder removes its email). When such a reminder falls due, each of the same people is emailed through the workspace's mail server (`ANUM_SMTP_*`, the path every system email takes: `notifications.send_with_retries`, with retries for transient failures), only if their email notifications are on (Settings, Notifications, Email), their **Calendar reminders by email** switch is on too (`calendar_email`, the calendar-only switch, on by default, migration `0174_calendar_email_prefs`), ANUM knows their address (team profile, else the accepted invitation's email) and the [Contacts directory](contacts.md#enforcement) does not mark that address do not contact by email. Each person gets the email in their own language: **Language of calendar reminder emails** in the same settings (`calendar_email_language`: English, Arabic, or English then Arabic, the default). The pass writes all three versions when it claims the email and the send picks the person's one, so a change of language applies to the next email. ANUM has no other per-person language record (the web keeps its language in the browser, and a phone's language is only known for push), so the choice is made here. The message is plain text: the title, when it starts in the event's zone, the place and how to join (the meeting link stays in ANUM), and how to turn these emails off or change their language. Web: Settings, Notifications shows the switch after Email (with a warning while Email is off) and the language choice (`apps/web/src/NotificationSettings.tsx`, `lib/push.ts`, test `test/push.test.ts`); Flutter: the same two controls under Email delivery (`apps/mobile/lib/features/settings/settings_screen.dart`, `calendar_email_strings.dart`, test `test/notification_email_test.dart`). Each email is claimed in `calendar_reminder_email_sends` (event, occurrence, minutes, person) in the reminder pass's transaction and sent after it committed, so it goes once even when the pass runs twice or in two processes; the claim records `sent`, `failed` or `skipped` with the reason (never the address or the title), and `calendar.reminder_emailed` is audited the same way. Without a mail server nothing is claimed. Attendees who are not workspace members are not emailed by the reminder (an email to them is an outward action that goes through the email tools and their approval).

## Storage

Migration `0045_reports_calendar` adds `calendar_events` (attendees as a JSONB list) under forced RLS on `tenant_id` and `workspace_id`, with checks on kind, status and `ends_at > starts_at`. Migration `0167_calendar_recurrence` (expand only) adds `all_day`, `timezone`, `recurrence`, `recurrence_until`, `skipped`, `reminders`, `series_id` (a child row's series, `on delete cascade`) and `recurrence_start`, the table `calendar_reminder_sends` (forced RLS, the lifecycle purge policy) and the `anum_maintenance` discovery grants above. Migration `0171_calendar_sync_depth` (expand only) adds `sync_error`, `sync_attempts`, `email_reminders` and `occurrence_responses` to `calendar_events` and the table `calendar_reminder_email_sends` (forced RLS, the lifecycle purge policy). No other role is granted anything. With `ANUM_REPOSITORY_BACKEND=memory` events are lost on restart.

## Sync

An owner connects a Google (Calendar API v3) or Microsoft 365 / Outlook.com (Microsoft Graph) account to the workspace, on the web or on the phone. Its primary calendar syncs both ways; other calendars of the account the owner chooses are read into ANUM ([Choosing calendars](#choosing-calendars)). Everything a provider returns is untrusted data and goes through the same validation as an event typed by a person.

### Setup

Each provider needs an OAuth client the owner registers; nothing is required and a provider without one is shown as "not set up" with the names of the missing settings. Settings are read from the environment (`.env.example` files hold placeholders only; secrets go in the secret store, never in git).

| Setting | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `ANUM_CALENDAR_GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID`, `ANUM_CALENDAR_GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET` | Google OAuth client. Empty: the inbox's `ANUM_EMAIL_GOOGLE_*` client is used, so one Google app can serve both (add the scopes `openid`, `email`, `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.events` and `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.calendarlist.readonly`) |
| `ANUM_CALENDAR_MICROSOFT_CLIENT_ID`, `ANUM_CALENDAR_MICROSOFT_CLIENT_SECRET`, `ANUM_CALENDAR_MICROSOFT_TENANT` | Microsoft (Entra ID) client and tenant (`common` by default; the inbox's values when empty). Delegated permissions `offline_access`, `User.Read`, `Calendars.ReadWrite` |
| `ANUM_CALENDAR_PUBLIC_API_URL`, `ANUM_CALENDAR_WEB_URL` | Public URLs; empty uses `ANUM_EMAIL_PUBLIC_API_URL` and `ANUM_EMAIL_WEB_URL` |
| `ANUM_CALENDAR_SYNC_INTERVAL_SECONDS` | Background sync interval (default 300; `0` turns it off; PostgreSQL only) |
| `ANUM_CALENDAR_DAYS_BACK`, `ANUM_CALENDAR_DAYS_AHEAD` | The window that syncs (30 days back, 365 ahead) |
| `ANUM_CALENDAR_APP_RETURN_URL` | Where a connection started on the phone returns after the provider's sign-in (default `com.anum.app:/calendar-connected`, the app's own scheme; an app scheme only, fixed on the server) |

The redirect URI to register with the provider is `<public API URL>/api/v1/calendar/sync/oauth/callback` (the Sync tab shows it). On Kubernetes the client ids go in `config` and the secrets only in `secrets.app` (the chart refuses `ANUM_CALENDAR_*_CLIENT_SECRET` in config); on the Oracle VM they are in `infra/oracle/.env`.

### Connecting

`POST /calendar/sync/oauth/{google|microsoft}/start` (owners, `calendar:manage`) returns the consent URL with PKCE and an encrypted `state`. The provider sends the browser to the unauthenticated `GET /calendar/sync/oauth/callback`, which only stores the returned code (encrypted) on the pending request and redirects to `/?calendar_connect=<id>#calendar`. The web page then calls `POST /calendar/sync/oauth/complete` as the signed-in owner who started it: the code is exchanged once (the request is consumed first), the account is read (`email`), the access and refresh tokens are stored encrypted with `ANUM_SECRETS_KEY` and a first sync runs. A provider that returns no refresh token is refused with the steps to fix it. Connecting the same account again reconnects it.

| Route | Who | Does |
|---|---|---|
| `GET /api/v1/calendar/sync` | `calendar:read` | Providers (configured, missing settings, scopes), the redirect URI, `can_manage` and the connections with their health, `fix` text and last summary |
| `POST /api/v1/calendar/sync/oauth/{provider}/start` | owners | Body optional: `{"client": "phone"}` sends the provider's return to the app instead of the web. Answers `authorization_url`, `expires_at` and `request` (the id to finish with) |
| `POST /api/v1/calendar/sync/oauth/complete` | the owner who started it | `{"connection": <request>}`: 409 while the provider has not sent the browser back, 400 when the person declined, 410 when expired, 404 when already used |
| `POST /api/v1/calendar/sync/connections/{id}/sync` | owners | One sync pass now |
| `GET /api/v1/calendar/sync/connections/{id}/calendars` | owners | The account's calendars fresh from the provider (id, name, primary, can_edit, color) with which sync, and `max_extra` (10) |
| `PUT /api/v1/calendar/sync/connections/{id}/calendars` | owners | `{"selected": [ids]}`: the calendars to sync besides the primary one ([Choosing calendars](#choosing-calendars)) |
| `DELETE /api/v1/calendar/sync/connections/{id}` | owners | Revokes ANUM's grant at the provider (where it has an endpoint), deletes the tokens and keeps the events in ANUM as ANUM-only events. Nothing is deleted at the provider |
| `POST /api/v1/calendar/events/{id}/invitations` | the event's creator or an owner | Asks for the event's invitations ([Invitations](#invitations)) |

Audited: `calendar.connect.started`, `calendar.connect.denied`, `calendar.connect.failed`, `calendar.connection.created`, `calendar.connection.reconnected`, `calendar.sync.run`, `calendar.connection.deleted`, `calendar.connection.calendars_chosen`, `calendar.invitations.requested`. `calendar.connect.started` records whether the web or the phone started it.

### Connecting on the phone

The phone uses the same flow, so the client secret and the PKCE verifier stay on the server and the phone holds nothing but the request id. **Calendar, Connections, Connect Google Calendar** calls `start` with `{"client": "phone"}`; the encrypted `state` then carries that the phone started it. The phone opens the consent page in the system browser (never a web view). After consent the callback stores the code as before and answers a small page (no-store, a CSP that allows nothing but its own inline style) that sends the browser to `ANUM_CALENDAR_APP_RETURN_URL?calendar_connect=<request>` at once and offers an **Open ANUM** button for browsers that ask first. That link only brings the app to the front: Android has an intent filter for exactly `com.anum.app:/calendar-connected` on the main activity (AppAuth keeps `/oauth2redirect`), iOS already registers the scheme, and Flutter's own URL routing is off on both (`flutter_deeplinking_enabled`, `FlutterDeepLinkingEnabled`), so no link can open a screen. When the app is back in front it calls `complete` with the request id it kept; a sign-in that has not come back yet (409) keeps the screen waiting quietly, and **I have signed in** tries again by hand with the reason shown. Only the owner who started the request can finish it, once. A declined, expired or used request says so and the person starts again; a browser that cannot open says that. The native wiring is in `apps/mobile/tool/configure_native.dart` (checked by `test/configure_native_test.dart`).

### Choosing calendars

The primary calendar always syncs both ways. An owner can also choose up to 10 other calendars of the same account (shared, team or holiday calendars) under **Choose calendars** on the web Sync tab or the phone's Connections screen. The list is read fresh from the provider (Google `calendarList`, which needs the `calendar.calendarlist.readonly` scope; Graph `/me/calendars`); a Google connection made before that scope was added answers 409 with the fix: disconnect and connect again. The choice is stored on the connection (`calendar_connections.calendars`, migration `0182_calendar_choices`) with each calendar's name, colour and whether it can be edited there.

Other chosen calendars are **read only**. Each sync pass reads each one's events in the sync window, series expanded (Google `calendars/{id}/events` with `singleEvents=true`, Graph `/me/calendars/{id}/calendarView`), and makes ANUM's copies match: new ones added, changed ones overwritten, and when the whole window was read, the ones gone removed (at most 1,500 per calendar; above that nothing is removed in that pass). A copy is linked to the connection with the provider id `<12 hex of the calendar id>~<event id>` and `source_ref` `calendar_copy`; like an event organised by someone else it follows the provider and is never sent back, so a local edit stays in ANUM until the provider changes the event. A calendar no longer chosen loses its copies in ANUM at once; one newly chosen is read in the same request. A calendar that cannot be read is named with the provider's reason in the pass's summary (`calendar_errors`) without failing the other calendars; the summary also counts `calendars_read`. New ANUM events still go to the primary calendar.

### How events sync

* **Incremental.** Google uses `events.list` with the stored `nextSyncToken`; Graph uses `calendarView/delta` and the stored `@odata.deltaLink` (only its token is kept; requests always go to the fixed Graph path, and a link to another host is refused). A sync token or delta link only reports changes inside the window it started with (Google gets `timeMax`, Graph an end date), so the window is renewed with a full sync once it ends more than a margin (30 days) behind the moving horizon: an event that later comes into range is picked up. An expired token (HTTP 410) starts a full sync again. Events longer than 14 days or without a valid end are skipped and counted.
* **Series from Google.** Google is read with `singleEvents=false`: a series comes as its master (an RRULE in `recurrence`) and the instances changed or cancelled on their own. When the rule is inside ANUM's subset (`calendar_providers.parse_google_recurrence`: one RRULE, no RDATE or EXRULE, no parts such as BYMONTH or BYHOUR; UNTIL in UTC becomes the last local day; EXDATE lines are removed occurrences) the master becomes one ANUM series in the master's zone, a cancelled instance goes into its `skipped` list and a changed one becomes a changed occurrence (a child row linked to the instance id). A rule outside the subset falls back to the series' instances (`events/{id}/instances`, at most 50 such series a pass), each stored as its own event, and a series held for it before is removed. The single events an older sync stored for a series' instances (ids `<master>_<start>`) are removed once the series arrives; a cursor from before this change starts one full sync. All-day events come as ANUM all-day events (in the event's zone, UTC when Google names none).
* **Series from Microsoft.** Graph's `calendarView/delta` lists occurrences only, never the series master, and names a deleted occurrence only by its own id. So the client reads each series the delta names (an occurrence's `seriesMasterId`): the master with `GET /me/events/{id}` (its `recurrence` pattern and range) and the series' instances in the window with `GET /me/events/{id}/instances` (paged). Why this and not something else: v1.0 has no list of a master's cancelled occurrences (`cancelledOccurrences` and `exceptionOccurrences` are beta only, which ANUM does not use), and the delta has no master to read. The pattern becomes ANUM's rule (`calendar_providers.parse_graph_recurrence`: `daily`, `weekly` with its days, `absoluteMonthly`, `relativeMonthly` on one weekday, `absoluteYearly` on the series' first day; the range's end date, count or no end) in the series' zone (`recurrenceTimeZone`, else `originalStartTimeZone`; an IANA name, or a Windows name read through the CLDR table in `calendar_providers.WINDOWS_ZONES`; an all-day series in UTC). The instances changed on their own (`type: exception`) become changed occurrences, and an occurrence the rule makes that Graph no longer lists was removed in Outlook and goes into the series' `skipped` list (`graph_removed_occurrences`). That inference is guarded: nothing is taken as removed when Graph lists no instance at all, only occurrences at least 14 days inside the window's edges count (an occurrence moved out of the window on its own would otherwise look removed), all-day occurrences are compared by their day, and when any instance has an original start that ANUM's reading of the rule does not make, the readings disagree and the series stays as single events. A pattern outside the subset (`relativeYearly`, a yearly date other than the first day, a relative month on several weekdays, a week starting on another day than Monday for a rule with an interval and several days, an unknown zone) also keeps the series' occurrences as single events, as before. When the delta reports a removed id that matches nothing ANUM holds, the series ANUM holds for that connection are read again the same way, so an occurrence deleted in Outlook from a series ANUM sent is removed (skipped) in ANUM; a master that is gone (404) removes the ANUM series unless it has unsent edits. At most 50 series are read in one pass; the others wait in the cursor (at most 500) and are read in the next passes. A series ANUM made itself is never replaced by its single occurrences, and when the master only changed because an occurrence was removed (the same run, title, place, people and description preview), only the removed occurrences are applied, so ANUM's own attendees and description stay. The single events an older sync stored for a series' occurrences are removed when the series arrives (by the instance ids read with it), and a cursor from before this change (no `"v": 2`) starts one full sync. An occurrence of a series ANUM holds (`type: occurrence`) is never imported again on its own.
* **ANUM's own series and all-day events to the provider.** A series is sent with each provider's own fields: an RRULE line for Google (`calendar_providers.google_rrule`, the start and end as wall-clock times in the series' zone, UNTIL as the end of that local day in UTC, a date for an all-day series) and a `recurrence` pattern and range for Graph (`graph_recurrence`: `daily`, `weekly` with `daysOfWeek`, `absoluteMonthly`, `relativeMonthly` with `index`, `absoluteYearly`; the range ends on a date, after a count, or never, in `recurrenceTimeZone`). All-day events are dates (Google's end date is the day after; Graph `isAllDay`). An occurrence removed in ANUM is deleted at the provider, inside the sync window or not: the skipped starts are read in groups (a gap over 31 days starts a new group; the 20 groups nearest to now on each send), the series' instances around each group are read and the matching ones deleted; one already gone is not listed, so this repeats safely; an occurrence changed in ANUM is written on its instance (found by its original start, then remembered by id; Google gets `status: confirmed` so a restored occurrence comes back); this and following shortens the series there and sends the new series; deleting the series deletes the master. A changed occurrence whose series has not reached the provider yet waits for it. A synced single event cannot be made repeating or all-day (422; add a new event in ANUM only instead). Reminders are ANUM's own and never sync. Microsoft cannot repeat on the last day of a month: such a series is refused at once with that reason.
* **Failed sends.** A send that fails keeps the provider's short reason on the event (`sync_error`) and stays `pending_push`; each failed send counts (`sync_attempts`), and after five in a row, or at once when the provider has no way to express the change, the event is shown as not sent (`sync_failed`) and sync passes leave it alone. Its creator or an owner presses **Send again** (`POST /events/{id}/sync/retry`), which starts the count again and sends it at once; any edit does the same. A connection that needs reconnecting is not counted against the event.
* **External ids.** Each linked event stores the provider's event id (`external_id`), its ETag or change key, when ANUM and the provider last agreed (`synced_at`) and `sync_state`: `synced`, `pending_push` (waiting to be sent) or `local`. One provider event maps to one ANUM event per connection (a unique index).
* **ANUM to the provider.** A new event goes to the oldest working connection unless the person turned "Also add to ..." off (`sync: false`). The change is saved first, then sent at once; if the provider cannot be reached it stays `pending_push` and the next pass sends it (the Sync tab shows the error and what to do). Cancelling a synced event removes it at the provider and unlinks it; deleting one removes it at the provider first and is refused (with the reason) when the provider cannot, so nothing is lost silently.
* **Background job.** With PostgreSQL a scheduler syncs every connection each `ANUM_CALENDAR_SYNC_INTERVAL_SECONDS`. Discovery runs as the narrow `anum_maintenance` role (migration `0092_calendar_sync`: ids, scope and status of connections that do not need reconnecting; never a token or an address); each sync then runs as the application role inside that connection's tenant and workspace RLS context. A lease keeps two passes from running for one connection. A connection that needs reconnecting is skipped until an owner reconnects it.
* **Microsoft specifics.** Graph tells attendees about every change the organiser makes, so a change to an event that has people is held (`sync_state` stays `pending_push`, counted as `held` in the sync summary) until an owner approves the event as it now is under [Invitations](#invitations); Google changes go out silently because ANUM sends `sendUpdates=none`. Updating an event that carries a Teams meeting never replaces its body, so the join details survive (a description edit on such an event stays in ANUM).
* **Events organised by someone else** follow the provider: a local edit stays in ANUM until the provider changes the event again.

### Conflicts

When an event changed in both places since the last sync, the later change wins (ANUM's `updated_at` against the provider's update time) and the provider wins a tie. The losing side is overwritten. A provider deletion removes the ANUM event unless it has unsent edits, which win and are sent as a new event. Counts of conflicts won by each side are in the connection's last summary (Sync tab and the `calendar.sync.run` audit entry of a manual sync). Echoes of ANUM's own writes are recognised by the ETag and do not count as changes.

### Invitations

ANUM never gives a provider an attendee list on its own: a provider sends invitation emails to attendees, which is an outward action. An event is created and updated at the provider without attendees (and without notifying anyone). To invite people, a member or the creator presses **Ask to send invitations** (`POST /calendar/events/{id}/invitations`), which creates a task paused for approval on the high-risk tool `calendar.send_invitations` (always asks, whatever the autonomy level). The approval shows the calendar, title, times, place and the exact addresses, bound to a payload hash. Only an owner can approve, and only with the hash that was displayed (a wrong hash is refused with 409). On approval the tool re-checks that the event and attendees still hash to the approved content (any edit after the request makes it fail and send nothing), then sends the attendees with notification on. After that, edits keep sending to the approved invitees; adding or changing attendees, the time, title or place makes `invitations_pending` true again and needs a new approval. Cancelling or deleting an event whose invitations were approved cancels it at the provider with notification. Attendees that came from the provider already hold their invitation there, so an imported event is not "pending". Attendees stay in ANUM, with the replies the organiser records, and provider replies update them on the next sync.

### States and fixes

| State | What the person sees |
|---|---|
| Provider not set up | "Not set up" with the missing setting names; no Connect button |
| Nothing connected | "No calendar connected yet" and the Connect buttons (owners) |
| Connecting | The page finishes the connection after the redirect, with a status line, then lists the account; a denied or expired request says to press Connect again |
| Sync error | The provider's short error, "Press Sync now ..." and the Sync now button; events stay readable and edits stay queued |
| One event not sent | The event's sync chip says it was not sent (or is waiting after a failure), with the provider's reason, what to fix and Send again for its creator or an owner; others are told who can |
| Reconnect needed | The grant was revoked or expired: the provider's text and "Connect ... again"; Sync now is disabled until then |
| Viewer or member | The status and each event's sync line, without Connect, Sync now or Disconnect |

Tokens never appear in responses, logs or audit records. `ANUM_SECRETS_KEY` rotation covers them (`python -m anum_api.rotate_secrets`, report key `calendar_connections`).

## Not done yet

- Other chosen calendars are read only, and a pass reads each one's whole window again (no per-calendar sync token); room calendars, attendee free/busy and meeting-room booking are not read.
- Google conference links are read, but ANUM does not create Meet or Teams meetings for new events.
- Connecting from the phone was tested against fakes and widget tests, not yet on a real phone with a real Google or Microsoft account (the device checklist in [Mobile](mobile.md)).
- New events go to the oldest working connection of the workspace (no per-event choice of which calendar).
- No drag to reschedule on the web, and no clicking an empty slot to add an event there (New event opens the form).
- From the mockups, not built: attendee availability (a scheduling assistant), meeting recordings, attachments and related records on an event, because the calendar API does not read free/busy or link records to events.
- Microsoft 365 series whose pattern is outside ANUM's subset, whose zone ANUM cannot name, or whose instances do not match ANUM's reading of the rule still arrive as their single occurrences ([How events sync](#how-events-sync)); an occurrence removed in Outlook within 14 days of the sync window's edges is not inferred as removed until it is further inside. Google series whose rule is outside ANUM's subset arrive as their instances.
- A series with more than 20 separate groups of removed occurrences removes the 20 groups nearest to now at the provider on each send; the others follow on later sends of the series.
- Reminders reach the creator and members whose team profile email is invited; there is no per-person switch to follow someone else's event.
